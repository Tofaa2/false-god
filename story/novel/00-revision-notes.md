# THE UNFINISHED: Revision Notes

This is a review of the treatment (`../01-the-unfinished.md`) before novelization: what was cheesy, what was thin, and what changed. The novel in this folder is the canonical version. Where it disagrees with the treatment, the novel wins.

---

## How to read the novel as a game script

- **Each chapter is one playable segment** (a level, a hub visit or a cutscene). Chapter order is play order.
- Prose is close third person on Wren, past tense. Everything Wren sees, the player sees.
- **The guardian's speech is in italics with no quotation marks.** Only Wren can hear it, unless it's fully manifested. This maps directly to a distinct VO treatment and subtitle style.
- Gameplay is marked with blocks like this:

> ◆ **GAME** · What the player controls, prompts, mechanics, boss rules, and what the novel compresses.

- The novel follows **one canonical route** through Act 3's choices. Where the game branches, a GAME block says so.

## Cheesy or weak: what changed

### 1. The death-count line was too gamey
**Before:** in the last scene, the guardian quotes the player's real death count.
**Problem:** it breaks the fiction at the most important moment and reads as a designer's trick. Critics would call it clever, which is the wrong reaction at a deathbed.
**Now:** **tally marks.** Wren's bedroom wall is covered in thousands of faint pencil tallies, made over fifteen years, and Wren assumes the guardian is keeping score of their failures. In the game, a new pencil tally appears near the respawn point **every time the player dies and gets up.** Neither the book nor the game explains them. In the last scene Wren asks, *"What were you counting?"* and the answer is *"Mornings."* The reveal is the same, but it's diegetic. Players who noticed the marks get the payoff; players who didn't still get the line.

### 2. The guardian becoming "Wren at nine" was an inner-child cliché
**Before:** it shrinks to child height and takes Wren's nine-year-old face.
**Now:** it shrinks back into **what it was before it changed: a small, ordinary bird**, cleanly inked. Babies' gods take a first shape, and in Lowmarrow children are often named for it. Wren's god was a little bird, so their mother named them **Wren**. This is planted in Chapter 2 (the mother) and paid off on the steps. Oren's old nickname for Wren, "little bird", now means something.

### 3. "Everyone clapped" was a slow-clap trope
**Before:** Wren lights the lamp, colour floods out to the hills, and the crowd applauds.
**Now:** nobody laughs, and nobody claps. Someone at the back says *"Oh,"* softly. Colour comes back to **the square only**, and spreads over the spring, in the epilogue. A smaller miracle is a more believable one.

### 4. Severine's "Still Accord" was a generic world-ending ritual
**Before:** the villain plans one great Stilling of every god on the continent, using Wren as the "nib."
**Problem:** it turns a quiet, personal story into an apocalypse plot, and it makes Severine a cartoon in the last act.
**Now:** Severine's goal is grounded, and **she's partly right.** After the midpoint, the underdrawing spreading from Wren is genuinely unmaking places. Severine comes to Lowmarrow to **Still Wren completely**, both as mercy and to stop the spread. She doesn't believe that giving the gods back is shrinking it. The final fight isn't about saving the world. It's about Wren getting one more night: long enough to give Oren back his god and climb the steps.

### 5. Wren's decision to give the gods back came from nowhere
**Before:** after the diagnosis, Wren simply "decides" to return the gods.
**Now:** it's **discovered**. In Corran, Ambrose shakes Wren's hand, and because the gap is now so wide, his god **slips back through it by itself.** Gods want to go home. Wren sees what happens to Ambrose, and sees the underdrawing around them shrink. Every return after that is a choice, and it's always asked, never imposed.

### 6. Everyone said yes to their god coming back
**Before:** every return was a joyful-painful reunion.
**Now:** **Juniper says no.** Stilled, she has been on stage every night for months without dreading it. Wren respects her choice. The Veiled stays inside Wren, and it widens the gap for the rest of the game. Not everyone wants it back, and the story doesn't judge her.

### 7. The midpoint mechanism was handwaved
**Before:** "Wren's own power, focused inward like a lens."
**Now:** the Order's rite needs the guardian held still. Wren uses the four stolen gods **to pin their own guardian down while the Stillers erase it.** It's the darkest image in the book, and it's the same thing Wren has been doing to other people's gods all of Act 2.

### 8. The lamp backstory was told, never shown
**Before:** the Midwinter Lamp at nine only appeared in the character notes.
**Now:** it's a **playable flashback** in Chapter 14, triggered when the laughter in the Velle theatre breaks through. The player carries the taper up the same steps they'll climb at the end, and falls. The fall can't be avoided.

### 9. Wren had no history outside the guardian
**Now:** Wren's mother, **Ada**, was Stilled when Wren was eleven. She's pleasant, calm and gone. Wren remembers being relieved that she stopped crying at night. That's why Wren wants Stilling, and why they're afraid of it.

### 10. Lines that were too quotable
| Before | Problem | Now |
|---|---|---|
| "It runs out when I do." | Grand, romantic-poster | "Then you'll be at the end of the lane, and you'll know which way's home." |
| "I'm not waiting for you to be better. I'm just waiting for you." | Sounds like a quote on a mug | "I'm not keeping the cup for whoever comes back fixed. It's for whoever comes back." |
| "You're offering it to someone who'll wear my face." | Slick | "Then who'd be living it?" |
| "I'd rather it was mine." | Slogan | "I'd rather be there for it." |
| "I was so angry with you. I was so proud of you." | Sentimental symmetry | Cut. The guardian never says it was proud. The count says it. |

### 11. Kept, because it already works
Oren's *"I think this is good."* Tess's letter. Ambrose's *"Look how many."* Juniper's *"What do you think the sash is?"* Hal's names. The midpoint silence. The guardian fighting on Wren's side. Mira's tea, thread and stitches. Saying the voice out loud.
