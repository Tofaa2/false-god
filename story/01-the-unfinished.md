# THE UNFINISHED

> *A young person who has spent their life hunted by their own god travels to the Order that promises to silence it, and learns, too late to save themself, what silence costs.*

**Ending:** Tragedy. Wren dies. The guardian and Mira, the person who loves them, are with them at the end.
**Length target:** 10–14 hours
**What it argues:** The voice says trying is pointless. Wren dies, and trying was not pointless.

---

## Part I: The People

### Wren (24)

Labourer at the Callow Dye Works in **Lowmarrow**, a mill town on the edge of the Fade. The dye barely takes any more, and every year the bolts come out paler. Wren's job is to stir the vats and pretend not to notice.

At 17 Wren washed out of the Order's novitiate. The Order's assessors couldn't Still Wren's god. The file says **UNBINDABLE** in red ink, and it was the most attention anyone had paid to Wren in years. Wren went home and has been "fine" for seven years.

**On the surface:** dry, quick and funny in a way that ends conversations before they get close. Good at seeming fine. Remembers everyone's tea order, and only one person has ever asked about theirs.

**Underneath:** Wren believes that being hard on yourself is the same as being responsible. If the voice ever stopped, Wren is sure, they'd become someone worse.

**Want:** the voice gone.
**Need:** to stop treating their own mind as an enemy to be killed.
**Flaw:** Wren will do anything to stop feeling, including taking things that aren't theirs.

**Visual design:** Wren is the only inked character in the game whose outline **isn't closed**. There's a small gap at the left shoulder where the line simply doesn't meet, as if the artist lifted the pen early. Nobody comments on it, and most players won't notice it until Act 3, when the gap has grown down the whole left side.

### The Guardian

Wren has never named it. The Order's file calls it *Lowmarrow Subject 11*.

**Design:** twice Wren's height and made entirely of **pencil underdrawing**: construction lines, erased marks, the circles and guidelines an artist sketches before inking. It has no face, only the faint crosshair an artist draws to place one. It moves in stepped animation at a **lower frame rate than everything else**, so it's always slightly out of sync with the world, like something pasted into it.

**Voice:** Wren's own voice, a little older and flattened. It never shouts and never hurries. It's patient, sometimes funny, and **never wrong about facts**. It is wrong about what the facts mean.

**What it believes:** Wren was born with a small, ordinary god. It became *this* at age nine, on the night of the Lowmarrow Midwinter Lamp.

Every winter a child is chosen to carry the flame up the square to light the great lamp. At nine, Wren was chosen. Wren tripped on the steps. The lamp oil caught on the cobbles. Nobody was hurt, but the square laughed, the way a crowd laughs from nerves, and the festival was called off that year.

That night, in bed, Wren's god spoke its first full sentence:

> **"We won't do that again."**

Everything since has followed from that sentence. The guardian isn't trying to kill Wren out of hatred. It is trying to make sure Wren never again stands where a crowd can watch them fail. Over fifteen years that logic has narrowed to its most extreme conclusion, and the guardian has never once looked at the conclusion directly.

**How it evolves on screen:**

| Stage | Form |
|---|---|
| Prologue / Act 1 | Rarely seen whole. A shape in the corner of the frame. Construction lines at the edges of rooms that shouldn't be there. |
| Act 2 | Visible and growing. It **wears inked pieces of the gods Wren has taken**: the Hound's lantern, the Ninth Draft's glass plating, the Veiled's veil, all crudely traced over its pencil body. |
| After the midpoint | No body at all. It's in the world: rubbed-out walls, the sky turning to grid paper, Wren's own shadow. |
| Act 3 | It re-forms smaller each time Wren gives a god back. By the end it's **the height of a nine-year-old**, and its lines are clean. |

### Oren Tamsin (52)

Lowmarrow's lamplighter. Big, soft-spoken, with a constant low-level worry about everything. He hasn't slept a full night in thirty years, because his god, **the Lantern-Hound**, never stops keeping watch.

The Hound is enormous, scruffy and drawn in warm orange ink, with a lantern where its heart should be. It barks at every noise. It checks the locks four times. It is exhausting, and Oren loves it the way you love an old dog that's ruined your furniture.

Oren was the young lamplighter who stamped out the fire on the night Wren dropped the lamp. He told the crying nine-year-old, *"Happens to everyone, little bird."* Wren doesn't remember. Oren does.

**What happens to him:** in the prologue, Oren's god is driven feral by Wren's guardian, and Wren takes it to survive. Oren becomes Stilled. He sleeps through the night for the first time in thirty years. He smiles. He says, *"I think this is good,"* in the tone of someone reading it off a card.

He follows Wren on the journey because, as he says pleasantly, he doesn't feel strongly about going home.

He's the player's companion and the story's conscience. **He never accuses Wren of anything, and that makes it worse.**

### Mira Callow (26)

Bookkeeper at the Callow Dye Works, and the owner's daughter. She keeps the ledgers for a business that's dying a little more every year as the dye stops taking, and she has never once pretended otherwise. She's practical, stubborn, quick to laugh and slow to give up. She has warm, slightly smudged linework, like a drawing that's been handled a lot.

**Her god is the Mender**: a patient, long-fingered figure made of thread and darning wool, with a needle for a finger. It fixes things. Torn sleeves, cracked cups, the fence at the dye works, the neighbour's roof. When Mira is anxious, it fixes faster. When she's frightened, it tries to stitch shut anything that's coming apart, whether or not it should be.

**Mira and Wren.** For two years they've been almost something. Mira is the only person in Lowmarrow who has ever asked how Wren takes their tea. (Milk, no sugar, left to go cold because Wren always forgets it's there.) Every morning she leaves a cup on the vat-room windowsill. Every morning it goes cold. Neither has said anything. Wren is certain that the moment Mira sees them properly, she'll leave. The guardian has been saying so for two years.

**Her arc is the love story's arc.** She begins the game wanting to **fix** Wren. She ends it understanding that she can't, and choosing to **stay** anyway. The game's central argument (control versus coexistence) plays out between two people as well as between Wren and the guardian. The Mender's stitches are how the player sees that happen.

**What she is not:** a cure. Loving Wren doesn't make the guardian quieter, doesn't stop the gap widening and doesn't save Wren's life. **Her love doesn't solve anything. It keeps Wren company.** The story is careful about this, because "love fixes depression" is a lie that hurts real people.

**Why she can't hear the guardian:** in this world everyone can see gods, but most of the time only the host can hear what their own god says. Mira can see the tall pencil figure behind Wren. She has no idea what it's been telling them. That gap is where the love story lives: Wren will eventually have to say the guardian's words out loud to her.

### Mother Severine Voss (60s)

**Stillkeeper of the Order of the Still Hand.**

Severine Stilled her own god at twelve. When people ask why, she answers simply: *"It told me I'd be better off gone, and I was a child, and I believed it."* She has the cleanest linework in the game: perfect, closed, unshaded, like a stained-glass saint.

She is not a hypocrite and she does not lie. She is warm, unhurried and attentive. She remembers Wren from the novitiate and is honestly glad to see them. She believes suffering is the only real evil, and that a quiet, faded world is a mercy the living are too frightened to accept.

**What she wants:** to use an unbindable god as a **nib**, the point of a pen, to perform **the Still Accord**: one Stilling that reaches every god on the continent at once. End all suffering in an afternoon.

Lowmarrow Subject 11 is the strongest unbindable god the Order has ever recorded.

> **SEVERINE:** I'm not offering you happiness, child. Nobody can offer that. I'm offering you quiet. Ask yourself honestly which one you came here for.

### The hosts

Each region is built around one person and their god. Each is a short story in its own right.

| Host | Who they are | Their god | What the god is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Oren Tamsin** | Lamplighter, Lowmarrow | The Lantern-Hound | Vigilance that never rests |
| **Tess Morrow** | Courier who never stays anywhere more than one night | The Courier | Avoidance |
| **Ambrose Pell** | Master glassblower who hasn't finished a piece in eleven years | The Ninth Draft | Perfectionism |
| **Hal Brennick** | Ferryman whose village drowned | The Undertow | Grief |
| **Juniper Aske** | The most famous actor in Velle | The Veiled | Shame |

## Part II: The Shape of the Game

### How it plays

Semi-linear third-person action. The core loop:

1. Enter a region shaped by one person's god.
2. Explore. The guardian rewires routes, rewrites signs and taunts you.
3. Learn that person's story in pieces, from them, from the people around them and from the environment.
4. The guardian drives their god feral. Fight it as a boss whose mechanics come from what it is.
5. **Take** the god. Gain its ability, which opens new routes and new combat options.
6. The guardian adapts to how you played: it counters your most-used tool and **says so out loud**.

**Every time Wren takes a god, the gap in their outline widens a little.** This is never mentioned until Act 3.

### Abilities

| Ability | From | Combat | Exploration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Watchlight** | The Lantern-Hound | Pulse that reveals enemies through walls and briefly shows attack telegraphs early | Shows the guardian's edits: rooms it changed glow faintly |
| **Threshold** | The Courier | Draw a door in the air and step through it (short teleport, invulnerable during transit) | Seal or open passages; take shortcuts |
| **True Edge** | The Ninth Draft | A perfectly timed attack does triple damage and shatters projectiles | Cut through glass and crystal barriers |
| **Undertow** | The Undertow | Heavy slam that slows everything in an area | Sink through water to drowned paths |
| **Unwatched** | The Veiled | Become unseen; enemies lose track of you | Walk past sentries; see things that only exist when no one is looking |

### Death and respawn

When Wren dies, the screen washes to paper-white, then Wren is redrawn lying down and gets up. The guardian always has a line:

> *"Nobody would have noticed if you'd stayed down."*
> *"Again? You're very committed to something that isn't working."*
> *"I'd let you rest, you know. That's all I've ever wanted."*

**The game counts every death silently.** That number pays off in the final scene.

### The red thread and the letters

**The red thread.** When Wren leaves Lowmarrow, Mira gives them a spool of red thread from the Mender. One end stays tied to the dye works gate. The thread unwinds behind Wren for the whole journey.

On the map and in the world, the thread is **the one route the guardian can't edit**. It can rub out bridges, rewire corridors and turn roads into loops, but the red thread always runs, unbroken, back toward Lowmarrow. When the guardian has turned a level into a maze, following the thread backward is always a way out. The player will learn to rely on it before they understand why it works.

**The letters.** At each lamp (rest point), the player can write to Mira. Mira's replies wait at the next waystation.

- Wren's letters are **composed with the guardian**. The player picks lines, and the guardian supplies most of the options, all variations on "I'm fine." There is usually one honest option. It's always the hardest to find and the shortest.
- Mira's letters are funny, specific and full of Lowmarrow news: the dye works, Oren's empty house, the neighbour's goat. She never asks Wren to come home. She signs every one the same way: *"There's a cup on the windowsill. — M."*
- What Wren writes changes what Mira writes back. If Wren only ever writes "I'm fine," Mira's replies get gentler and more careful. When Wren chooses an honest line, Mira's next letter answers it directly.

This system is cheap (text and a few UI screens) and it carries the whole love story through Acts 1 and 2, while the two of them are apart.

### The guardian's adaptation (the director system)

The guardian tracks how you play: most-used ability, dodge direction, preferred range, healing habits, routes. **At each region boundary it picks one habit and counters it**, and tells you which:

> *"You dodge left. You always dodge left. Did you think I wouldn't notice? I notice everything you do."*
> *"You love that door trick. Let's see you use it when every door goes to the same room."*
> *"You heal the moment you're scratched. Like someone's going to take it away from you."*

Counters last one region, then rotate. Each one is visible, temporary and beatable. Its model of you is also slightly **wrong**, and attentive players can exploit that.

## Part III: The Story

### PROLOGUE: LOWMARROW

#### Scene 1: Morning

*Black screen. A sound: a cheap clock ticking too loudly.*

*Fade in on a small room drawn in pale, tired ink. Grey light. Wren is in bed, on their side, facing the wall. The camera holds. Nothing happens for long enough that the player tries the controls.*

*The stick does nothing. Wren doesn't move.*

*A pencil line draws itself in the corner of the room: a curve, then another, the beginning of something tall. It doesn't finish.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Nobody will notice if you don't get up.

*Beat.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** You know that, don't you. The vats will get stirred. Callow will dock your pay and won't even be annoyed about it. It'll be a relief for everyone. A day off from pretending you're worth the wage.

*A prompt appears in the bottom corner, small, written in the same pencil as the guardian:*

**[ GET UP ]**

*The player presses it. Nothing happens. Wren doesn't move.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** See.

*The player presses again. Wren's hand twitches. Pressing again, Wren's eyes open. Holding the button, Wren slowly sits up. It takes eleven real seconds of holding. It should feel heavy, but not cruel: long enough to notice, short enough not to resent.*

*Wren sits on the edge of the bed. Out the window, a poster on the wall opposite reads THE ORDER OF THE STILL HAND, RECKONING TODAY, ALL WELCOME, and someone has drawn a little smiling sun on it.*

> **WREN:** *(to no one)* Morning.

> **THE GUARDIAN:** You're thinking about the Reckoning.

> **WREN:** I'm thinking about breakfast.

> **THE GUARDIAN:** You're thinking about going down there and asking them again. Seven years later. Standing in line in front of the whole town so the assessor can tell you, in front of everyone, that you're the one thing in Lowmarrow they can't fix.

*Wren pulls on their boots.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** We won't do that again. Remember?

*This is the first time the game uses the line. Players won't know what it refers to yet.*

#### Scene 2: The town

*Gameplay begins. A short walk through Lowmarrow, functioning as a tutorial for movement, the camera and talking to people. The town is pale but still drawn: washing lines, the dye works chimney, the great Midwinter Lamp in the square on its iron post, unlit and rusted, out of season.*

*Every person has a god beside them. A baker's is a fat, anxious hen that keeps counting the loaves. A child's is a scribble the size of a kitten that keeps darting off and being called back. Two old men are arguing, and their gods are arguing too, in exactly the same posture.*

*People greet Wren. Wren is charming with all of them, quick with a joke, gone before the conversation turns. The game lets the player feel how good Wren is at this.*

*Mira is at the dye works gate, ledger under her arm, waiting. The Mender is beside her, darning a hole in the gatepost's rope.*

> **MIRA:** You're late.

> **WREN:** I'm early for tomorrow.

> **MIRA:** *(not smiling, then smiling)* I was about to come and knock. *(she holds out a cup)* Milk, no sugar. Drink it before it goes cold, for once in your life.

*Wren takes it. The player can drink it or not. Either way, Wren holds it a moment too long.*

> **MIRA:** You look tired.

> **WREN:** I always look tired. It's my face.

> **MIRA:** Mm. *(she looks past Wren's shoulder, at the empty air there, the way people look at a god)* Is it bad today?

*Wren has never answered that question. The dialogue wheel offers three options, all of them supplied in the guardian's pencil: "I'm fine." "Just tired." "Don't worry about me." There is no fourth option. Not yet.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** *(only Wren hears it)* She asks because she's kind. She'd ask the goat. Don't mistake it for anything.

*Whatever the player picks, Mira nods as if she's heard that answer a hundred times, which she has.*

> **MIRA:** All right. *(beat)* The cup'll be on the sill.

*This is the first of many cups. It also sets up the game's first line. "Nobody will notice if you don't get up" was already a lie. Someone was about to come and knock.*

*Oren is at the base of the Midwinter Lamp with a ladder, checking the iron. The Lantern-Hound is circling the ladder, barking at a cat, at the wind, at nothing.*

> **OREN:** Morning, Wren. You're up early.

> **WREN:** Couldn't sleep.

> **OREN:** Ha. Join the club. *(nods at the Hound)* He had me checking the shutters four times last night. Four. Thought he heard foxes.

> **WREN:** Were there foxes?

> **OREN:** There's always foxes, according to him. *(softer)* You going down to the Reckoning?

> **WREN:** Me? No. Just walking.

> **OREN:** Mm. *(beat)* No shame in it if you were.

*The Hound stops barking. It turns and looks at Wren, or at the space just behind Wren's left shoulder, and its hackles rise.*

> **OREN:** Hey. Hey, easy. It's just Wren.

*The Hound keeps staring at the empty air behind Wren.*

#### Scene 3: The Reckoning

*The town hall. A long line of townspeople. At the front, under a white canopy, an Order assessor in white robes sits at a small desk. Behind him, two Stillers stand with their hands folded.*

*Wren is in the line. The game never shows the decision to join it. One moment the player is walking past the hall, and the next the camera cuts to Wren standing in line, as if the decision happened somewhere the player wasn't allowed to see.*

*The line moves. People reach the front and the assessor rests two fingers on the forehead of each person's god. Most are sent home with a pamphlet. One young man is gently led behind a curtain. When he comes out, his god walks behind him silently, smaller, its colour gone grey. He looks rested. His mother is crying and he doesn't understand why.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** *(close, quiet)* Look at him. Look how calm he is. That could be you. All you'd have to do is let them try one more time. And when they fail, everyone in this room will know that you're the thing that couldn't be fixed.

*Wren reaches the front.*

> **ASSESSOR:** Name?

> **WREN:** Wren. Of Callow's. I was... I was in the novitiate. Some time ago.

*The assessor checks a ledger. His finger stops. He looks up at Wren with something like recognition, and something like interest.*

> **ASSESSOR:** Lowmarrow Subject Eleven.

*A few heads in the line turn.*

> **ASSESSOR:** Would you show me your god, please?

> **WREN:** It doesn't... it doesn't really come out.

> **ASSESSOR:** Then I'll go to it.

*He reaches two fingers toward the empty air behind Wren's left shoulder.*

*The pencil lines arrive all at once.*

#### Scene 4: The first attempt

*This is the game's first set piece, and it must be terrifying.*

*Every construction line in the hall draws itself at once: perspective lines across the floor, grid lines over the walls, the guidelines underneath every object in the room. The world shows its scaffolding. The assessor's hand is caught in a pencil circle and erased, cleanly, from the wrist down, and he screams, though when the camera returns to him later the hand is there, drawn back badly.*

*The guardian stands up out of the space behind Wren, all the way up, twice Wren's height, construction lines folding into a body. The crosshair where its face would be turns toward the crowd. Toward the crowd looking at Wren.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Don't look at us.

*The hall's geometry folds. The floor tilts forty degrees. The ceiling beams draw themselves down as bars. Gameplay: an escape sequence, Wren running across a hall the guardian is actively redrawing. A doorway is rubbed out as Wren reaches it. A staircase is drawn upward into nothing.*

*Wren reaches the square. The guardian follows, not running, simply erasing the distance.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Stop. Stop running. Everyone's watching. Everyone's watching you run. Do you know what you look like?

*It is closing in. The camera is low and the guardian fills the top of the frame.*

*The Lantern-Hound slams into it from the side.*

*Oren's god, all orange ink and burning heart, has its jaws on the guardian's arm. Oren is running across the square shouting the Hound's name.*

*The guardian looks down at the Hound with what reads, even without a face, as curiosity.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Oh. You're a worrier.

*It puts one pencil hand on the Hound's head. The guardian's construction lines run down into the Hound's body. The Hound's eyes go white. Its lantern flares. It turns around to face Wren, and it's growling.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** He knows. He's always known. Something's wrong with you. Everyone can tell. He's been waiting thirty years for the thing in the dark, and here it is.

#### Scene 5: The Lantern-Hound (boss, tutorial)

*The Hound is a tutorial boss: big, fast and readable. Charges, bites, a lantern-burst. The player learns dodge, attack and parry. Oren is at the edge of the arena the whole time, shouting the Hound's name, begging it to come back.*

*When its health hits zero, the Hound drops on its side. Its lantern gutters.*

*A prompt appears. It is the same pencil as the guardian's, and later in the game the player will realize the guardian is the one offering it:*

**[ TAKE IT ]**

*There's no alternative button. The Hound is getting up. It's going to kill Wren. Wren takes it.*

*Wren's hand goes into the Hound's chest and closes around the lantern. The orange ink runs up Wren's arm, over the shoulder, and is absorbed. The Hound comes apart into lines. For one frame, the gap at Wren's left shoulder is visibly wider.*

*Across the square, Oren stops shouting.*

*He stands there. His shoulders drop. His face goes smooth.*

*He walks over, slowly, unhurried, and looks down at Wren with an expression of mild, pleasant concern.*

> **OREN:** Are you hurt?

> **WREN:** Oren. Oren, I'm sorry, I didn't, it was going to...

> **OREN:** It's all right. *(he listens to something, the way you listen for a sound that's stopped)* It's very quiet.

*He looks up at the sky. He smiles a little.*

> **OREN:** I think this is good.

*Behind them, the guardian is gone. Somewhere just out of shot, a pencil line is erasing itself.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** *(only Wren hears it)* Look at you. You couldn't even ask for help without hurting someone.

*Title card. Hand-drawn, wobbling slightly in stepped animation:*

**FALSE GOD**

### ACT ONE: ESCAPE

*Act 1 is about running and about wanting the cure. Wren leaves Lowmarrow for the Stillhouse in the north. The Order will try once more. If even they can't do it, Wren doesn't know what's left.*

*Oren comes along. When Wren asks why, he thinks about it for a long time.*

> **OREN:** I don't really feel strongly about staying.

*That line stays with Wren for the rest of the act.*

#### The gate

*Night. Wren is leaving before anyone wakes. Oren is waiting by the road with a pack, pleasant and ready. And Mira is at the dye works gate, in her coat, with a bag.*

> **MIRA:** I'm coming.

> **WREN:** You're not.

> **MIRA:** Father can keep the books for a month. He'll get them wrong, but he can keep them.

> **WREN:** Mira. *(Wren gestures at Oren, smiling politely at the moon)* Look at him. I did that. I didn't even mean to and I did that. That's what happens to people who stand near me.

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Tell her. Tell her what you'll do to her. You'll take the Mender out of her the same way, and she'll stand there smiling, and you'll have to look at it every day.

> **MIRA:** It wasn't you. I saw it. That thing — *(she looks past Wren's shoulder)* — that thing did it.

> **WREN:** That thing is me.

*It's the first time Wren has said it. Mira doesn't have an answer. The Mender does: it reaches out and starts, without asking, to darn the frayed cuff of Wren's coat. Wren lets it.*

> **MIRA:** *(quietly)* Then let me come anyway.

> **WREN:** No.

*A long pause. Mira takes something from her pocket: a spool of red thread. The Mender ties one end to the dye works gate in a neat, tight knot.*

> **MIRA:** Then take this. *(she presses the spool into Wren's hand)* It'll unwind as you go. If you ever want to come back, it'll show you the way.

> **WREN:** It's a spool of thread, Mira. It's going to run out by the end of the lane.

> **MIRA:** It's the Mender's. It runs out when I do.

*Wren leaves. The red thread unspools behind them down the lane, a thin bright line across the pale road, the only vivid colour in the shot.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** She'll cut it within a month. Everyone does.

*She doesn't. The player will be able to see the thread running back toward Lowmarrow on every map for the rest of the game.*

#### Oren as companion

*At rest points, which are lamps Oren lights, Wren can sit with him. He's pleasant and patient. He answers every question. He has no opinions. He sleeps deeply every night, and Wren, who sleeps badly, lies awake listening to him. Through Act 1, Oren notices small things he used to care about and doesn't:*

> **OREN:** There's a crooked lamp there. *(he looks at it)* I used to hate that. Crooked lamps. I'd go back and fix them at night. *(beat)* I don't mind it now.

*The player's healing system is tied to these lamps. Healing charges are refilled by Oren's lamps and by people Wren has helped along the road. The guardian has something to say about that:*

> *"He lights them because he doesn't know what else to do with his hands. You did that to him. And you warm yourself at it."*

#### Region 1: The Tollway and Tess Morrow

*A long road of inns and waystations. Wren meets **Tess Morrow**, a courier who moves every night and never unpacks. She's funny, fast-talking and exhausting. Her god, **the Courier**, is a long-legged figure made of folded letters, always on the edge of leaving.*

*Tess is carrying a sealed letter she's been carrying for two years. She won't open it, she won't deliver it, she won't throw it away. It's from a friend she abandoned during the worst year of that friend's life. She never found out what happened.*

*Wren and Tess get on immediately. They're the same kind of person. The game should let the player enjoy their friendship for a while; this region has the most banter in the game.*

*Then the guardian finds the Courier.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** *(to Wren)* She's like you. She runs from everything that matters. Let's see how fast.

**Boss: The Courier.** *An avoidance god. It never attacks directly. The fight is a pursuit across a three-floor waystation inn. The Courier seals doors behind itself, collapses staircases, and drops parcels that unfold into paper proxies that fight for it. If Wren chases directly, it escapes forever. The player has to use Watchlight to read its routes and cut it off, cornering it where there are no exits.*

*When it is cornered, the Courier stops. It doesn't fight. It holds out its letter-hands like someone who's been caught and is relieved about it.*

**[ TAKE IT ]**

*There is a choice here, and it's the game's first real moral test: **the player can walk away.** If they walk away, the guardian drives the Courier feral again within seconds, and the fight resumes with the Courier now attacking directly, far more dangerous. The second time, there's no walking away. The game is honest: Wren cannot yet stop this, but the game remembers who tried.*

*After the taking, Tess is Stilled. She sits on the inn steps. She takes out the letter and opens it, the first time in two years, reads it all the way through, folds it and puts it on the step. She feels nothing about it.*

> **TESS:** Huh. She's fine. She married a cooper. *(beat)* That's nice.

*She walks off. She leaves the letter on the step.*

*Oren picks it up and puts it in his coat. He doesn't know why. The player will see that letter again.*

#### Region 2: Corran and Ambrose Pell

*Corran is a glassblowing city, all furnaces and greenhouse domes, one of the last vivid places on the map. **Ambrose Pell**, the most celebrated glassblower of his generation, has not finished a piece in eleven years. Every piece is broken one step before completion. His workshop is a cathedral of shards.*

*His god, **the Ninth Draft**, is beautiful: an armoured figure of layered glass, polishing itself constantly, never finished.*

*Ambrose is kind to Wren, and sad. He tells Wren he's booked a Stilling at the Stillhouse next month. He's exhausted. He just wants to make something.*

> **AMBROSE:** I'll be honest with you, I don't know which of us is going to make the work after. Him or me. *(the Ninth Draft polishes its shoulder)* I suppose it doesn't matter, as long as something gets finished.

**Boss: The Ninth Draft.** *A perfectionism god. Its glass armour only breaks on **perfectly** timed hits. Sloppy hits heal it, layering new glass over the crack. But it spends long, beautiful phases polishing itself, and it's vulnerable then. The fight teaches patience. Attack only when you're sure. Learn its rhythm.*

*The guardian, watching, as the Ninth Draft's armour heals itself again:*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** I like this one. It knows. It knows that if you can't do it right, you shouldn't do it at all.

*After the taking, Ambrose is Stilled. Within a week, which the game shows as a short montage when Wren passes back through Corran later, he finishes forty pieces. They're flawless and lifeless. He shows Wren, proud in a flat way.*

> **AMBROSE:** Look how many.

#### Letters home

*Through Act 1, the letters are where the love story lives. A typical exchange, from the lamp after the Courier fight:*

*The player opens the letter screen. The guardian has already written most of it, in pencil, waiting to be inked:*

> *Dear Mira,*
> *The road is fine. Oren is fine. I'm*
> **[ fine. ] [ keeping busy. ] [ sorry about the cup. ]**

*Hidden lower in the list, shorter and in Wren's own ink, is one more option:*

> **[ I took another one today. She opened her letter and felt nothing. I watched her do it. ]**

*If the player sends only the guardian's lines, Mira's reply at the next waystation is warm and careful, and it's all news: the dye works lost another contract; her father has started talking to the vats; Oren's house is empty and she's been feeding his cat; the Mender fixed the church bell and now it rings flat.*

> *There's a cup on the windowsill. — M.*

*If the player sends the honest line, Mira answers it, first thing, before any news:*

> *I don't know what it's like to do what you're doing. I'm not going to pretend I do. But you watched her. You could have looked away and you didn't. I don't think the thing behind your shoulder would have watched.*
> *Write anyway. Write when it's bad. Especially then.*
> *There's a cup on the windowsill. — M.*

*The guardian, reading over Wren's shoulder:*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** She's being kind. It's a letter. Letters are easy to be kind in. Wait until she has to look at you.

#### End of Act 1: The White Road

*Wren arrives at the Stillhouse. The final stretch of road is fully inside the Fade, nearly blank paper with the faintest sketches of trees. Here the player sees the Stilled: whole villages of them, pleasant and working and grey.*

*Wren's gap now runs from shoulder to elbow.*

*Severine meets Wren at the gate personally.*

> **SEVERINE:** Wren. *(she takes Wren's hands warmly)* I hoped you would come back. You were the most interesting student I ever failed.

---

### ACT TWO: CONTROL

*Severine cannot Still Wren's god. She tries, gently and seriously, and the guardian simply isn't there to be touched. It's everywhere and nowhere, like a draft from an open window.*

*But she's seen something. Every god Wren has taken has gone quiet inside them. In fact, she tells Wren, Wren may be the first person in the Order's history able to Still a god **without** the Order's rites, by sheer force.*

*She proposes an arrangement. There are feral gods out there doing terrible harm, gods the Order can't reach. Wren can. Bring them in, and in return, the Order's best minds will find a way to Still Wren's guardian for good.*

*Wren agrees. Wren is **good** at this.*

#### The tone shift

*Act 2 should feel **great** to play. Wren has four abilities now and combinations open up. Threshold into True Edge into Undertow: door, perfect cut, slam. The combat becomes stylish and fast. Music turns from lonely strings to percussion. Wren starts winning, and the player feels the power fantasy fully.*

*Wren's dialogue changes too. More confident, more clipped. Wren starts using Order vocabulary: "subject", "rites", "settled".*

*The guardian grows. Every god Wren takes, the guardian puts on a traced, inked copy of a piece of it: the Hound's lantern hanging in its ribcage, glass plating on its shoulders. It's becoming as heavily armed as Wren.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Look at us. Look how strong we are now. Nobody laughs at us any more.

*This is the most dangerous line in the game, because it's said warmly, and because it's true.*

#### The letters stop

*Somewhere in Act 2, Wren stops writing.*

*The game doesn't announce it. The letter option at the lamps is simply greyed out, and if the player selects it, the guardian answers instead of the screen opening:*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** We don't need to write to her any more. We don't need anything now. Isn't that a relief?

*Mira's letters keep coming. They wait at every waystation, and they pile up in Wren's inventory, a counter on the item: 3, 6, 9. The player can open them. When they do, the game shows only the first line before Wren folds the letter and puts it back.*

> *Wren — the thread's still going. I check it every morning. So I know you're*
> **[ Later. ]**

> *You don't have to write back. I just want to say that the cup*
> **[ Later. ]**

> *I asked the Mender if I'm being stupid and it fixed my bootlace, which I think means*
> **[ Later. ]**

*The red thread still runs back toward Lowmarrow on the map. In Act 2 it's the only warm colour on Wren's side of the screen. Some players will notice that they've stopped following it. The guardian never mentions it. It doesn't have to.*

#### Region 3: The Drowned Parish and Hal Brennick

*Severine sends Wren to the coast. A ferryman, **Hal Brennick**, has a feral god that is drowning the coast road. His village flooded six years ago. He was the only survivor. His god, **the Undertow**, is a vast, slow, dripping figure made of seaweed ink and heavy rain.*

*This is the turn. **Wren goes there to take a god.** Not in self-defence. Not by accident. On a job.*

*Hal doesn't want to be Stilled. He tells Wren so, plainly, sitting in his boat in the drowned church.*

> **HAL:** It's not killing me. It's just heavy. They were my whole life, that village. It's supposed to be heavy. *(he looks at the Undertow, which is crying quietly into the nave)* What would it say about them, if it weren't?

*The player can leave. They can turn around, tell Severine the god wasn't feral, and go home. The game lets you leave the region.*

*But the guardian whips the Undertow up anyway, the moment Wren turns to go. Every time. Until Wren fights it.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Oh, we're above this now, are we? We're the merciful one? You've done this twice already. It gets easier. Let it get easier.

*The player cannot win Hal's freedom. They can only choose to be the one who fights. This is intentional and it's the point: in Act 2, Wren's power always seems to arrive with the guardian's permission.*

**Boss: The Undertow.** *A grief god. The arena gets **heavier** the longer the fight goes on: movement slows, dodges recover more slowly, the screen gradually floods. Commit early, or learn to fight well while dragged down. Fighting it well means accepting the weight, not escaping it.*

*Hal, Stilled, rows his boat out across the parish, over the roofs of his drowned village, smiling slightly, and doesn't look down once.*

#### Region 4: Velle and Juniper Aske

*Velle, the theatre city. Wren is now practically an Order agent, given rooms and a white sash.*

*Here Wren meets **Juniper Aske**, the city's greatest actor, beloved and celebrated and so ashamed of herself that her god is forbidden from being seen: **the Veiled**, a shape that only exists out of the corner of the eye.*

*Juniper is the only host who sees through Wren. In a dressing room, mid-conversation:*

> **JUNIPER:** You're doing a performance too. *(she paints her eyebrow, watching Wren in the mirror)* I'd know. You're doing "the one who has it handled." It's not bad. You hold your hands too still, though.

> **WREN:** I'm not performing.

> **JUNIPER:** Darling, what do you think the sash is?

**Boss: The Veiled.** *A shame god. **Invulnerable whenever the player is looking at it.** Lock-on makes it vanish. It strikes when your back is turned. The player has to fight without lock-on, tracking it by sound and by the shadows it throws, catching it in mirrors in a backstage maze. The fight is staged during a live performance: the audience can be heard laughing at the show, and the laughter makes the Veiled stronger.*

*The guardian, during this fight, for the first time, sounds frightened:*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** They're laughing. Do you hear them? They're laughing at — no. No. Not at you. Not at you. Not again.

*After the taking, Juniper is Stilled. She goes on stage the next night. Critics call it the most technically perfect performance of her career. She doesn't read the reviews.*

#### Midpoint: The Quiet Room

*Wren returns to the Stillhouse with four gods inside them and a gap down the whole left arm.*

*Severine has done it. The Order has found a way: Wren's own power, all five taken gods, focused inward like a lens. With them, Wren can Still the guardian themself.*

*The Quiet Room is a vast white chamber at the heart of the Stillhouse, perfectly drawn. Not a smudge. Severine and the senior Stillers stand at the walls.*

> **SEVERINE:** It will come when it's threatened. It always does. When it comes, you'll know what to do. You're very good at this now.

*It comes.*

**Boss: The Guardian (first fight).** *This is the game's biggest fight to date, and it should be spectacular. The guardian wears every stolen god. It uses Wren's whole kit back against them: Threshold doors, True Edge counters, Undertow slams, Unwatched invisibility. It has been learning all game and it fights like Wren fights. It rearranges the Quiet Room's geometry, stretching floors into corridors, tilting walls.*

*Its dialogue during the fight is constant, a rising monologue that the player has been hearing in pieces all game:*

> *"Everything you do, I've seen. Every dodge. Every flinch."*
> *"You think you're stronger now. You're louder. It's not the same."*
> *"I was protecting you. I've always been protecting you. Do you know what they'd do, if they saw you as you are?"*
> *"Stop. Stop it. Wren, stop, I'm trying to—"*

*Wren wins. It's hard, and it should feel earned.*

*The guardian goes down on one knee. Its construction lines are fraying.*

**[ STILL IT ]**

*Wren puts a hand on its chest.*

*Every line it's made of erases at once, from the inside out.*

#### The Quiet

*Silence.*

*Not just narrative silence. **The game's audio stops.** No music, no ambient sound, footsteps silent, no UI sounds. The player can walk. That's all.*

*Wren walks out of the Quiet Room. Severine embraces Wren, and Wren feels nothing about it, and that's fine. The Stillers smile.*

*For the next fifteen to twenty minutes, the player plays Wren after the Stilling. It's the most important stretch of the game.*

- *Enemies on the road don't taunt; nothing does. Combat is easy. The guardian's adaptations are gone. Wren's abilities work perfectly every time.*
- *Wren doesn't speak unless spoken to.*
- *The colour leaves the screen. Slowly, almost unnoticeably, a little each minute, until the world is fully greyscale and the player realizes they don't know when it happened.*
- *Wren sleeps through the night. Players get a "rest" screen: a black frame and the word MORNING, with no getting up. Wren is simply up.*
- *Oren and Wren sit by a lamp. For the first time, they're the same.*

> **OREN:** You sleep now, too.

> **WREN:** Yes.

> **OREN:** Is it good?

> **WREN:** *(after a long pause)* I don't know how I'd know.

*That night, by the lamp, Wren finally opens the letters. All of them. There are fourteen.*

*Wren reads them aloud to Oren, in the post-Stilling voice: level, pleasant, unhurried. The player hears Mira's letters for the first time in Wren's voice, and in total silence, with no music under them.*

> **WREN:** *(reading)* "The dye took on the blue bolt today. Only a little. Father cried. I didn't tell him it's already fading." *(turns the page)* "There's a cup on the windowsill."

> **WREN:** *(next letter)* "Oren's cat had kittens in your boots. I've left them there. You'll have to come back and evict them yourself." *(turns)* "There's a cup on the windowsill."

*Fourteen times. The last letter is short:*

> **WREN:** *(reading)* "I don't know if you're reading these. I'm going to keep writing them. I want you to know something, in case it's the thing you need to hear and nobody's said it. I'm not waiting for you to be better. I'm just waiting for you." *(beat)* "There's a cup on the windowsill. M."

*Wren folds the letter.*

> **WREN:** That's nice.

*Wren puts the letters away and goes to sleep, and sleeps well.*

*Here the player should begin, without being told, to miss the voice. And to miss Mira, whose love is reaching Wren in full, and landing on nothing.*

#### The return

*Wren takes the road back south. It's the same road from Act 1, and it is visibly worse. The Fade has moved. Places that were vivid are sketches. Corran's furnaces are grey. People on the road who were loud and alive in Act 1 are quiet and pleasant.*

*Then Wren reaches a waystation, and the floor of the waystation is graph paper.*

*Then the walls are construction lines.*

*Then the sky is a grid.*

*The game's audio comes back all at once as a scratching pencil, loud, everywhere.*

*The guardian wasn't Stilled. Stilling erases a god's lines. But the guardian **is** lines, the underdrawing beneath everything Wren is, and when its body was erased, it went into the paper. It's no longer standing behind Wren. It's in the world around Wren, wherever Wren goes, and wherever Wren goes, the world turns to underdrawing.*

*And it's not talking. That's the worst part. For the first time in fifteen years, the guardian has nothing to say. It's simply everywhere, erasing.*

*Wren looks down. The gap in their outline runs from shoulder to hip.*

---

### ACT THREE: THE UNFINISHED

#### The diagnosis

*Wren goes back to the Stillhouse. Severine is waiting. She already knows. The Order's watchers have been tracking the spreading underdrawing.*

*She takes Wren into her study, a small, warm room, the only room in the Stillhouse with any clutter. She makes tea. She asks Wren to roll up their left sleeve.*

*The gap runs the whole length of the arm now. Inside the gap, where Wren should be, there is nothing: not skin, not ink, just paper. And at the edges of the gap, faintly, the player can see colours that aren't Wren's: the Hound's orange, the Draft's glass blue, the Undertow's green.*

> **SEVERINE:** You've been carrying five other people. *(gently)* No one is drawn to hold that much. It's in the grain now. It won't come out.

> **WREN:** How long?

> **SEVERINE:** Not long. A season, perhaps. Less, if you keep fighting.

*Silence.*

> **SEVERINE:** There is one way. Let me finish what we started. Let me Still the rest of you. Not just the god. All of it. If there's nothing left in you to fight, the gods you're carrying will settle. They'll go quiet. You'd live. A long time, I think.

> **WREN:** As what?

> **SEVERINE:** As what Oren is. As what I am. *(she smiles, and means it)* It isn't so bad, Wren. I know you think it is. I promise you it isn't. There's no fear in it.

> **WREN:** You're not offering me my life. You're offering it to someone who'll wear my face.

> **SEVERINE:** And you're refusing a long, quiet life for a short, loud one. *(she puts her cup down)* I've spent forty years watching people make that choice and regret it. I won't stop you. But I need to tell you what I need, because you deserve honesty from me.

*She tells Wren about the Still Accord. One Stilling, every god at once. No one ever again suffering the way she suffered at twelve, the way Wren has suffered since nine. All she needs is a god strong enough to serve as the nib. Wren's guardian, spread into the paper of the world, is now the strongest thing the Order has ever seen.*

> **SEVERINE:** I'll take it with or without your consent. I'd rather it was with. It would be a kind ending for you, Wren. Your suffering would end the world's.

*Wren leaves. Severine lets Wren go. She knows where Wren is going. Everyone goes home at the end.*

#### Mira at the gate

*Snow at the Stillhouse gate. And at the end of the white road, standing in it with her coat torn and her boots wrecked, is Mira.*

*She followed the thread. Three hundred miles of it, backward, from the dye works gate. The Mender is beside her, winding the thread up as she walks, and the spool in its hands is enormous.*

*She sees Wren. She doesn't run. She walks the last part, steadily, the way you walk toward someone who might bolt.*

> **MIRA:** You stopped answering.

> **WREN:** I know.

> **MIRA:** Fourteen. I counted.

> **WREN:** I read them.

> **MIRA:** *(beat)* When?

> **WREN:** After. When I couldn't — *(Wren stops)* I read them when it didn't matter, Mira. I read them when I couldn't feel them. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry.

*Mira looks at Wren properly for the first time in the game: the white sash, the stillness, the guardian's construction lines bleeding faintly through the snow around Wren's boots. Then she looks at Wren's left side, where the coat hangs strangely, and she sees the gap.*

> **MIRA:** What's that?

*Wren tells her. All of it. How long. The game plays the conversation in a single held wide shot, the two of them small against the white wall of the Stillhouse, with no music and the dialogue so quiet the player has to turn it up.*

*When Wren finishes, Mira doesn't say anything. The Mender moves first. It reaches out, needle-finger raised, and starts, very fast, to **stitch the gap closed.***

> **WREN:** Mira —

> **MIRA:** Let it.

*Red thread. Neat, tight, beautiful stitches, running down Wren's arm, pulling the edges of the line back together. Where the stitches close, colour comes back into Wren. For the first time in the game, Wren's outline is whole.*

> **MIRA:** *(her voice cracking, steady anyway)* There. See? There.

#### Stitches

*Mira travels with Wren for the rest of the game.*

*Every night, at the lamps, the Mender stitches Wren's gap closed. **Mechanically, the stitches are a temporary boost to maximum health**, and they tear a little in every fight. Players will start relying on them, the same way Mira does.*

*And every night they hold a little less. In the first region of Act 3, the stitches last the whole day. By the third, they tear by noon. Mira starts stitching more often. The Mender starts stitching faster, more frantically, double and triple rows. Its stitches get messy. The thread runs out and it unpicks Mira's own coat to make more.*

*The player watches someone they love try to fix something that can't be fixed. It's the same mistake Wren made, in a gentler form: **control**, born out of love instead of fear, but control all the same.*

*By the last region, the Mender is stitching things that aren't broken. It stitches Wren's sleeve to the bench by the lamp. It stitches the lamp-house door shut against a Stiller patrol that isn't coming. Mira's god is starting to go feral, the way every god does when its host is in crisis. Wren is the one who notices.*

*The guardian notices too. It's child-sized now. It watches the Mender work and says, very quietly:*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** She's trying to keep you. *(beat)* I know what that looks like.

#### What Act 3 is about

*Wren has a season. Maybe less. The question has changed from **how do I survive** to **what do I do with the time I have left.***

*Wren decides to give the gods back.*

#### Giving back (the core Act 3 mechanic)

*Wren travels back through every region, finding the Stilled hosts and returning their gods.*

**Each time Wren returns a god, Wren loses that ability.** *The player's kit gets smaller. Act 3 is the only act where the player gets **weaker** by doing the right thing. Every return is a real gameplay cost, and the player feels it in the next fight.*

**And there isn't enough time for all of them.** *The Fade is spreading faster. Severine's Stillers are hunting Wren. The game lets the player reach most of the hosts, but each return costs time, and the route through the map is a real decision. The order in which you return gods matters, because each lost ability closes certain routes.*

*Each returning is its own scene, and they are the emotional heart of the game. Two in detail:*

**Returning the Courier to Tess.** *Tess is working in a sorting office, Stilled, efficient and pleasant. Wren gives back the Courier. Folded letters flood back into the air around her. She staggers, grabs the counter, and the first thing she says is:*

> **TESS:** The letter. Where's the — I opened it. I opened it and I didn't — she married a cooper. She's alive. She's *alive* and I didn't even — *(she's crying)* Wren, I didn't even feel it. I read that she was alive and I felt *nothing.*

*Oren takes the letter out of his coat, the letter he's carried this whole game without knowing why, and hands it back to her.*

> **TESS:** *(reads it again)* Oh. *(laughing, crying)* Oh, she's so *happy.*

*She's going to go and see her friend. She's terrified about it. The Courier is already trying to run, and she grabs its paper hand and doesn't let go.*

**Returning the Undertow to Hal.** *Hal is in the boat on the drowned parish, rowing his slow, smiling circuit. Wren gives back the Undertow. The whole parish fills with weight: rain, slowness, groaning water. Hal stops rowing. He finally looks down, through the water, at the roofs of his village.*

> **HAL:** *(after a very long time)* There's the Mercer's. There's the school. *(beat)* I forgot. I *forgot* them, Wren. Six months I've been rowing over their roofs and I couldn't remember their names.

*He starts saying names. The game lets him. A long list of names, in Hal's voice, while the camera holds on the drowned roofs. It's heavy, the way it's supposed to be.*

#### The guardian, coming back

*Something happens each time Wren gives a god back.*

*The underdrawing around Wren pulls inward. The grid sky recedes a little. And the guardian **re-forms**, from the paper, from the world, back into a body. But each time, it's smaller.*

*After the first return, it's Wren's height. After the third, it's shorter than Wren. Its pencil lines start to clean up: fewer construction marks, simpler lines.*

*And for the first time since the Quiet Room, it speaks.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** *(after Tess)* You're giving them back. *(beat)* That'll make you weaker.

> **WREN:** Yes.

> **THE GUARDIAN:** They'll catch you.

> **WREN:** Probably.

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Then why are you —

> **WREN:** Because I took them.

*The guardian doesn't have anything to say to that. It's never had anything to say to that.*

*Through these scenes, the guardian's dialogue changes. It's still the same voice, still negative, but the negativity is turning into fear:*

> *"They're coming. I can hear them. We should run."*
> *"Don't go in there. Please. They'll see you."*
> *"Why are you — stop going toward them. Every time, you walk toward the people who'll hurt you."*

*And it starts, quietly, to protect Wren. A rubbed-out bridge in front of a Stiller patrol. A doorway drawn just in time. The player realizes the guardian's interference is now pointing **outward**.*

#### The night at Hollin's Lamp (saying it out loud)

*The last lamp before Lowmarrow. A ruined lamp-house on a hill with the town's faint lights below. Oren asleep. The Mender has finished another row of stitches and is already unpicking Mira's scarf for more thread.*

*Wren puts a hand over the Mender's needle.*

> **WREN:** Stop. Please.

> **MIRA:** It's nearly holding —

> **WREN:** It's not. It held for an hour today. *(gently)* You can't sew me shut.

> **MIRA:** *(furious, crying, all at once)* Then what am I supposed to *do?* I walked three hundred miles, Wren. I followed a *thread*. You don't get to — I'm supposed to *do* something.

> **WREN:** I don't know. *(beat)* Can I tell you what it says?

*Mira goes still.*

> **WREN:** You've seen it for years. Behind me. You never asked what it says. I'd like to tell you. I've never told anybody.

*She sits down.*

*This scene is interactive, and it's the heart of the love story. **The dialogue wheel is filled with lines the guardian actually said to the player in this playthrough**, pulled from the taunts, adaptations, respawn lines and letter-screen comments they really heard. The player picks them one at a time, and Wren says each one out loud to Mira, in Wren's own voice.*

*Mira answers every one. (Her responses are written per category of line: worth, failure, being seen, being left, being a burden.)*

> **WREN:** It says nobody would notice if I didn't get up.

> **MIRA:** I was about to come and knock. Every morning. I had my coat on. *(beat)* Every single morning, Wren.

> **WREN:** It says you only ask because you're kind. That you'd ask the goat.

> **MIRA:** I don't ask the goat how it takes its tea.

> **WREN:** It said you'd cut the thread within a month.

*Mira holds up her hand. The end of the thread is tied around her wrist. It has been the whole time.*

> **WREN:** It says you'll leave when it gets bad.

> **MIRA:** *(very quietly)* Is this not bad?

*The player can keep going as long as they want. The list contains every line the guardian said, and it might be long. The scene lasts as long as the player needs.*

*At the edge of the lamplight, the guardian sits listening. It's hearing its own words in Wren's voice, said to someone else, and answered. It looks smaller after every line. At the end:*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** *(barely audible)* They sound different. Out loud.

*Mira takes the Mender's needle-hand in hers. Slowly, together, they unpick the last row of stitches. The gap opens again, paper-white, edged with other people's colours. Mira looks at it for a long time and doesn't look away.*

> **MIRA:** All right. *(beat)* Then I'll just be here.

> **WREN:** That's not much.

> **MIRA:** It's what I've got.

*She kisses Wren. It's brief and clumsy, the way first kisses between people who've waited two years usually are. The guardian, child-sized, turns politely to face the wall. It's the first time in the game the guardian has done something funny on purpose.*

*They sleep side by side under the lamp. Wren doesn't sleep well. Mira doesn't either. They both stay.*

*The next morning, the prompt from the game's very first scene comes back:*

**[ GET UP ]**

*It takes just as long to hold as it did in the prologue. Nothing is cured. But this time, beside Wren's bedroll, there's a cup. Milk, no sugar. Still warm.*

#### Returning the Hound

*Oren's god is the last. Wren saves it for last, which the game enforces: the Hound will only come out of Wren when they're back in Lowmarrow. It's the god Wren took first, and the hardest to give back, because it's the reason Oren is still here: of the two people who stayed with Wren, he's the one who never chose to.*

*Lowmarrow. Midwinter. The town is deep in the Fade, almost blank paper. But the great Midwinter Lamp is still there on its iron post, and the townspeople have gathered in the square, quietly, because it's the night of the lighting and the Stilled still keep to their calendars.*

*Severine is there. The Still Accord will be performed here, tonight, using Wren's guardian, which has come home with Wren. Severine has brought the entire Order.*

*Before anything else, Wren gives Oren back the Hound.*

*The scene is quiet. They're by the lamp. Wren puts a hand on Oren's chest, and the orange ink runs out of Wren's arm, down Wren's fingers, back into him. The Lantern-Hound draws itself in, enormous, scruffy and alarmed. It immediately starts barking at everything. At the Order. At Severine. At the square.*

*Oren stands very still. His shoulders come up. His face creases. He lets out a breath that becomes a sob.*

> **OREN:** Oh, it's *awful.*

*He's crying. He's laughing. He puts his hand in the Hound's ruff.*

> **OREN:** It's so loud. I'd forgotten how loud. *(to Wren)* Thank you. *(beat)* It's awful. Thank you.

#### The final boss: Severine and the Stillness

*Severine Stilled her god at twelve. But the Order has a secret: a Stilled god isn't destroyed. It's put away. Severine kept hers. For fifty years she has carried it inside her, the way Wren has carried the stolen gods. It is **the Stillness**: a perfectly white figure with no lines at all, a shape cut out of the drawing.*

*She calls it out to begin the Accord.*

**Boss: The Stillness.** *It erases. Its attacks remove sections of the arena, turning cobbles to blank paper you can't stand on. It removes Wren's abilities temporarily with a touch. It fights in perfect, clean lines, inhumanly smooth animation in a game made of stepped animation.*

*And Wren has almost nothing. Most of Wren's abilities have been given back. The player goes into the last fight weaker than they've been since the prologue.*

**But Wren isn't alone.**

*The guardian, now child-sized, stands next to Wren. And for the first time, it fights **with** the player.*

*Every interference system the player has hated all game now targets the enemy:*

- *The guardian **rearranges the arena** against Severine. It draws walls between her and Wren and redraws erased cobbles back under Wren's feet.*
- *It **summons copies of every god Wren has ever fought**, the Hound, the Courier, the Ninth Draft, the Undertow, the Veiled, as pencil sketches that fight beside Wren.*
- *It **turns Severine's abilities against her**, exactly the way it turned Wren's against Wren in the Quiet Room.*
- *It **adapts to Severine's habits** and calls them out loud:*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** She always attacks from the left. She's been doing it for fifty years. *(beat)* I notice everything.

*And Mira is in the fight too, not as a fighter but as the Mender. Where the Stillness erases the square, the Mender **stitches it back**: red thread darning blank paper into cobbles again, under Wren's feet. Her stitches aren't trying to fix Wren any more. They're holding the ground Wren stands on. It's the same power that failed at the lamps, pointed at something it can actually hold.*

*Between them, the player has the guardian redrawing the world and the Mender stitching it together. The two forces that spent the game trying to control Wren, one out of fear and one out of love, now keep the floor under them.*

*This is the payoff to the whole game's central system. The thing that knew every weakness the player had is now pointed at someone else. The player should feel it: **I spent fifteen hours fighting this thing and now it's on my side.***

*Wren and the guardian win. Severine falls. The Stillness doesn't break. It simply sits down beside her, small, like a dog.*

> **SEVERINE:** *(from the ground, quietly, without anger)* You'd rather it hurt.

> **WREN:** I'd rather it was mine.

*Severine looks at her own god for the first time in fifty years. She doesn't say anything. The game doesn't tell us what she does next.*

#### The Lamp

*The Accord is broken. The Order scatters. The square is quiet.*

*The Midwinter Lamp is still unlit. The ceremonial taper is lying in the snow.*

*Oren picks it up and holds it out to Wren.*

> **OREN:** Nobody's lit it in years.

*Wren looks at it. Wren looks at the steps up to the lamp. The same steps.*

*The guardian, child-sized, standing beside Wren, takes a half-step back.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** *(very small)* Everyone's watching.

*They are. The whole square, Stilled and un-Stilled alike, Tess and Hal and Ambrose and Juniper among them if the player returned their gods, has turned to look.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** They'll see if you fall. They'll *laugh.* We said we wouldn't do this again. We *said.*

*Mira is at the foot of the steps. She doesn't offer to come up. She knows this isn't hers to carry. She takes the end of the red thread from her wrist and ties it to the bottom of the lamp-post's iron rail, in a neat, tight knot.*

> **MIRA:** *(to Wren, quietly)* I'm right here. It'll show you the way down.

*Wren takes the taper. Wren holds out the other hand.*

*The guardian looks at it.*

> **WREN:** Come on.

*The guardian takes Wren's hand.*

*Gameplay: the player walks up the steps. Slowly. It's the hardest walk in the game. Not mechanically. The steps are just steps. But the gap in Wren's outline is now so wide that Wren is visibly coming apart, lines lifting off into the air like ash, and every step the screen loses a little more. The guardian walks beside Wren the whole way.*

*Wren lights the lamp.*

*Light spreads out across the square. And with the light comes **colour**, real, saturated colour, the first in Lowmarrow for years, flooding out from the lamp in a circle that reaches the edge of the square and keeps going, into the streets, over the roofs, out toward the hills. Lines thicken. The Fade burns back.*

*The square doesn't laugh. Somebody, somewhere in the crowd, starts clapping. Then more of them.*

*Wren sits down on the top step.*

#### The last scene

*This scene must be written, acted, animated and scored with more care than anything else in the game.*

*Wren is sitting on the top step under the lit lamp. Lines are lifting off Wren into the light. Wren is mostly paper now, the colour fading from the edges in.*

*The guardian sits beside Wren. It's exactly the height of a nine-year-old now, and its lines are perfectly clean. For the first time, it has a face, and it is Wren's face, at nine.*

*It is scared. It's holding Wren's sleeve.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Get up.

> **WREN:** I don't think I can, this time.

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Get up. Get *up.* You always get up. *(its voice cracks)* Please.

> **WREN:** You used to tell me nobody would notice if I didn't.

*The guardian is quiet for a long time.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** I noticed.

*Beat.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** Every time. I noticed every time you got up. I counted. Five thousand, four hundred and seventy-nine mornings.

*It looks at Wren.*

> **THE GUARDIAN:** And today [N] times.

***[N] is the player's real death count.*** *Every time the player died and pressed the button and got back up, the guardian was counting. Every taunt at every respawn, "Nobody would have noticed", was a lie the guardian was telling while it kept count.*

> **WREN:** *(smiling, barely)* That many?

> **THE GUARDIAN:** You never stayed down. Not once. I was so angry with you. *(beat)* I was so proud of you.

*Footsteps on the steps. Mira climbs up, following the red thread, and sits on Wren's other side. Close. Her shoulder against Wren's good one.*

> **WREN:** You were supposed to stay at the bottom.

> **MIRA:** I did. It was cold.

*The Mender comes up behind her. Out of habit it reaches for Wren's gap, needle raised. Mira takes its hand, gently, and lowers it into her lap.*

> **MIRA:** Not tonight.

> **WREN:** *(to Mira)* It used to tell me you'd leave. When you saw.

> **MIRA:** I know. You told me. *(beat)* I saw.

*Wren looks at the guardian on one side, and at Mira on the other.*

> **WREN:** *(to the guardian)* You can stop guarding now.

> **THE GUARDIAN:** I don't know how.

> **WREN:** Me neither.

*Wren leans against the guardian. Or the guardian leans against Wren. They're the same height now, sitting. Mira takes Wren's hand. It is mostly paper, and she holds it anyway.*

> **WREN:** Stay anyway.

> **MIRA:** *(barely)* Wasn't going anywhere.

*The guardian stays.*

*The light takes Wren and the guardian at the same moment. Not an explosion. The lines simply lift, all together, into the lamp-light, and drift up into the colour. Mira holds on until there's nothing to hold. The game keeps the camera on her hand, closed around the space where Wren's was, for a long time.*

*Mira is alone on the top step, under the burning lamp, in a square full of colour. The red thread still runs down the steps to its knot on the lamp-post rail. Its other end lies beside her on the step: the spool Wren carried for three hundred miles, empty.*

*She doesn't cry yet. She sits. The Hound comes up the steps and lies across her feet. Oren sits on the step below.*

*Hold on the lamp. Hold. The sound of the square: people talking, quietly, for the first time in years, about nothing.*

*Cut to black.*

### EPILOGUE

*Spring. Lowmarrow, vivid and thickly inked, colour all the way out to the hills. The circle of colour is visibly larger than it was at Midwinter. It's been growing.*

*Morning at the Callow Dye Works. The vats are steaming, and the dye is taking: a bolt of cloth comes out of the blue vat **blue**, deep and real, the first in years. Mira's father is standing in front of it, weeping, again.*

*Mira is at the vat-room window. She's thinner. She looks like someone who has spent a winter grieving and is still doing it. The Mender is beside her, mending the Midwinter banners for next year, and it's working slowly. Its stitches are careful, and it doesn't hurry any more.*

*She sets a cup on the windowsill. Milk, no sugar.*

*She looks at it for a while.*

*Then she picks it up and drinks it herself, before it goes cold.*

*At the dye works gate, a knot of red thread is still tied to the post. Nobody has untied it. The Mender re-ties it every time the rain loosens it.*

*Evening. Oren walks his lamp round, the Hound bounding ahead, barking at a fox that is, for once, actually there. Oren is tired. He's always tired. He stops at a crooked lamp and straightens it. He passes the dye works, and Mira is on the step.*

> **OREN:** *(the question she used to ask Wren)* Is it bad today?

> **MIRA:** *(after a moment)* Yes.

*It's the honest answer. The one Wren could never give. She gives it plainly, and Oren nods, and sits down on the step beside her for a while, and neither of them tries to fix it.*

> **MIRA:** *(eventually)* Cup's on the sill, if you want one.

*He does.*

*At the edge of the square, a young woman is sitting on a doorstep with a newborn. The baby's god, brand new, a scribble the size of a kitten, is crawling over the doorstep, and its lines are pencil. Construction lines, unfinished, like a sketch someone has just started.*

*The mother looks worried. Oren stops.*

> **OREN:** First one?

> **MOTHER:** She's so loud. *(the scribble god is shrieking at a pigeon)* Is that... is that normal?

> **OREN:** *(looks at the Hound, which is barking at the same pigeon)* Oh, it's awful. *(beat)* You'll be all right.

*He lights the lamp beside her door, and moves on.*

*Credits. Over the credits: Wren's sketches, drawings in the margins of an Order novitiate's workbook. Mostly of birds. In one margin, a tiny drawing of a tall, scribbled figure next to a small one, holding hands, and a line in a child's handwriting:*

> *we will do it again*

## Part IV: Why This Ending Works

**The guardian's claim is disproved.** *Its argument for fifteen years was: don't try, because it's pointless. Wren dies, but Wren's last season gives five people their lives back, burns back the Fade and lights the lamp. The guardian itself, in its last line, admits the truth: every attempt mattered, and it was counting.*

**The death is not chosen and is not a release.** *Wren dies of what Act 2 did: control, taking, suppression. It's a consequence of the Order's philosophy, not of the guardian winning. Wren refuses Severine's offer to survive Stilled, but frames that refusal as choosing to be present for what's left, not choosing to die. And Wren's last word is "stay".*

**The death count line recontextualizes the whole game.** *Every respawn the player sat through, every guardian taunt, becomes evidence. No other medium could do this exact thing. It's the moment critics will write about.*

**The guardian is never simply killed.** *The game's violent "solution" at the midpoint makes everything worse. The real solution is non-violent, but it is achieved with everything the player learned through violence: the guardian finally becomes the player's best weapon instead of their enemy.*

**The love story supports Wren without rescuing them.** *Mira's love doesn't quiet the guardian, close the gap or save Wren's life. When she tries to make it do those things (the stitches), the story shows that as the same mistake Wren made, control, only gentler. Her arc goes from fixing to staying, the same move Wren makes with the guardian. That keeps the romance from claiming that the right person can cure depression, while still letting love matter enormously.*

**The love story is the evidence against the voice.** *Every specific thing the guardian says about Mira is disproved by something she does: the knock, the cup, the thread on her wrist, three hundred miles of walking, sitting on the top step. When Wren says the guardian's lines out loud, built from the lines this player actually heard, Mira answers them one by one. The player watches the critic's case lose, line by line, in a conversation.*

**The death has a witness who has to keep living.** *Mira is the reason the death reads as a loss and not a release. The game spends its epilogue with someone grieving: tired, honest, drinking the tea before it goes cold. The guardian once said Wren's absence would be "a relief for everyone." The epilogue is a long, quiet look at how wrong that was.*

**It's not tidy.** *Mira is grieving and will be for a long time. Oren is back to sleeping badly. Tess is terrified. Hal is grieving. Severine's fate is left open. The Fade isn't gone, just pushed back. The baby's god is pencil. Someone else will have this exact struggle.*

## Part VI: Production Notes

**Cheap things that do a lot of work:**
- *The midpoint silence section is just the existing game with audio and colour removed. Almost free, and it'll be the most discussed stretch.*
- *The guardian's copies of defeated gods are re-used boss assets, rendered in a pencil shader.*
- *The underdrawing world after the midpoint is a shader over existing levels: show the construction lines, desaturate, paper texture.*
- *The final boss's "guardian helps you" systems are the same interference systems pointed at a new target.*
- *Wren's widening outline gap is a single parameter on the outline shader.*
- *The love story through Acts 1–2 is **letters**: text, one UI screen, and a pick-a-line system. It's the cheapest romance you could build, and the distance helps it.*
- *The red thread is a spline from the player back to Lowmarrow, excluded from the guardian's geometry edits.*
- *The "saying it out loud" scene reuses the game's own dialogue log. The guardian lines the player heard are already tracked for the director system. Mira needs roughly five categories of reply, not one per line.*
- *The Mender's stitches are a decal on Wren's outline gap plus a temporary max-health buff.*

**Expensive things to protect:**
- *The final scene. Voice acting, animation and music for those three minutes are worth more than any single region.*
- *The return scenes in Act 3. They have to land, each one.*
- *The guardian's voice actor. They carry the game.*
- *Mira's voice actor, especially for the Hollin's Lamp scene and the top step. Underplayed is better than big.*
