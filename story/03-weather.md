# WEATHER

> *A Crown surveyor sent to condemn a house full of people whose gods are too loud for ordinary life discovers that the house is the only place on the continent where the world is getting brighter, and that her own god will erase it off the map to stop her from staying.*

**Ending:** Coexistence. Ilse lives. The guardian stays. Nothing is cured. The house survives, at a price.
**Length target:** 12–16 hours (hub-based)
**What it argues:** You don't defeat your mind. You learn to live in it, and you can't do that alone.

---

## Part I: The Premise in One Paragraph

The other treatments are about a single person and their god. This one is about a **household**. Its protagonist is older, functional and lonely in a way that looks like competence from the outside. Its central location is a hub that grows warmer as the game goes on. Its abilities are **borrowed** from friends, not taken from enemies. Its final boss can't be killed, only weathered. It's the gentlest of the four, and the one most likely to make someone who's lived with depression feel seen. It ends on an ordinary morning.

---

## Part II: The People

### Ilse Harrow (41)

**Crown Surveyor, Second Class.** Twenty years walking the edge of the Fade with a theodolite and a measuring chain, marking where the colour stops. The **Fade Line** she draws is used by the Crown to decide which districts are evacuated, and which are designated **Quiet Zones**, where the Order offers Stilling as "Fade protection".

She lives in a rented room in the capital. She's very good at her job. She's precise, dry, a little funny in a way people mostly don't notice. She was engaged once, years ago, and left without explaining. She hasn't really spoken to anyone outside work in four years. She considers this to be fine.

She functions. That's the point. She gets up, goes to work, does excellent work, comes home, eats something standing up, and lies awake. Nobody has ever worried about Ilse Harrow, because Ilse Harrow has never once looked like someone you'd worry about.

**Want:** promotion to Chief Cartographer. A desk in the capital. No more fieldwork. She is sure she'd be fine if only she were somewhere else.
**Need:** to stay somewhere.
**Flaw:** she believes that needing people is a failure of planning.

**Visual design:** Ilse is drawn in neat, careful, slightly thin ink, the linework of a technical drawing. Muted colours: slate, brown, dark green. Over the game, as she spends time at the Rest, her palette **warms** very gradually: a little more saturation each act. Players won't notice it happening until they see a screenshot from the start.

### The Margin (Ilse's god)

**Design:** a tall, thin figure made of **map annotation**: scale bars for limbs, compass roses for joints, crossed-out place names and tiny handwritten notes covering its body like tattoos. Where its face should be is a **blank cartouche**, the decorative frame on an old map where the title goes, empty.

It lives in the margins of Ilse's maps. When it's not manifest, it's the handwriting in the corners of every page she draws.

**Voice:** dry, factual, bureaucratic, quiet. It never exaggerates. It never insults. It just **reports**. It's the voice of someone reading a summary of your life back to you, in which every fact is accurate and every conclusion is terrible.

> **THE MARGIN:** Third evening in a row you've eaten standing at the window. Noting it. No conclusion drawn.

**Its power:** what Ilse draws on a map becomes a little more real. What she erases becomes a little less. The Margin edits her maps. A bridge rubbed out of the survey disappears from the world. A road redrawn three feet to the left leads somewhere else.

**What it believes:** that Ilse is a burden. That every person who's kind to her is being polite, and the kind thing, the considerate, tidy thing, is to remove herself from their maps before they have to ask. It's not trying to hurt her. It thinks it's being **courteous**.

**How it evolves:**

| Stage | Form |
|---|---|
| Act 1 | Barely visible. Handwriting in margins. Corrections in her notebook she doesn't remember making. |
| Act 2 | Fully visible, standing in corners of rooms, taller every time she's alone. |
| Climax | A **storm**: a weather system of isobars, wind arrows and rain made of crossed-out names, covering the whole sky above the house. |
| Ending | A small grey cloud in the corner of the kitchen ceiling. Still there. Overcast. |

### The household at Hob's Rest

**Hob's Rest** is an old coaching inn at the edge of the Fade, half a mile from where the colour stops. It's run by a retired Stiller as a house for **the Loud**: people whose gods are too much for ordinary life, who couldn't live in towns and wouldn't be Stilled. It's warm, cluttered, falling apart, loud in every sense, and the most vividly coloured place in the game.

**Agathe Lune (71).** Runs the Rest. A retired Stiller who, over thirty years, Stilled **two hundred and twelve** gods. Then one day she stopped, quit the Order, bought the inn, and started taking in the people the Order would have Stilled. She's blunt, warm, bossy, terrible at cooking and refuses to be thanked. Her god is **the Ledger**: a big, heavy book bound in old leather, which lists every god she ever Stilled, by name. She reads it every night. One name a night, out loud, in order. She's on her third time through.

> **AGATHE:** You don't defeat weather, love. You learn what to wear.

**Kit Ambry (34).** Former soldier, enormous, very quiet. He's the one who pulls Ilse out of the river. His god is **the Wall**: a vast, mossy, dry-stone wall that rises around him when anything is too much, shutting everything out, including the people who love him. Sometimes he's behind it for days. The household leaves food outside it.

**Margo Fenn (63).** Retired schoolteacher. Talks constantly, mostly apologies. Her god is **the Beetle**: a small, iridescent, endlessly apologetic beetle that says sorry for everything, all the time, before anyone's even upset. Margo is the kindest person in the game and believes she's an imposition everywhere she goes. She and Ilse recognize each other immediately and spend Act 1 being very politely careful with each other.

**Dov Halloran (22).** Painter. Brilliant, exhausting, sometimes awake for four days at a time. His god is **the Starlings**: a murmuration of ink birds that swirls and swoops and paints colour onto everything it touches. Dov has painted every wall of the Rest. Some of the paintings are extraordinary. Some were done at four in the morning on the third day without sleep, and they frighten him a little.

### The opposition

**Undersecretary Ewan Pryce.** Ilse's supervisor at the Crown Survey. Not a villain: a career administrator who has never been to the Fade and thinks of it as numbers. He wants the Rest district inside the Quiet Zone, because it makes his line straighter and his report tidier.

**Warden Tomas Callis.** The Order's regional Warden. A former student of Agathe's. He believes in the Order completely, and he loved Agathe completely, and he cannot understand what she did. He comes to the Rest twice in the story. The second time is to Still them.

## Part III: The Shape of the Game

### A hub, not a road

The other treatments are semi-linear journeys. This one is a **hub with expeditions**.

- **Hob's Rest** is the hub. It grows and changes across the game. Rooms get repaired. Dov paints more walls. Residents' dialogue changes with the story. Evenings at the Rest are where the story happens.
- **Expeditions** go out into the Fade on survey jobs: Faded towns, abandoned quarries, a lighthouse, a Stilled monastery. Each expedition is a dungeon with a boss.

### Ilse's tools

Ilse isn't a fighter. Her combat kit is her **surveying equipment**:

| Tool | Combat | Exploration |
|---|---|---|
| **Survey Staff** | A quarterstaff: sweeps, blocks, a planted-staff vault | Measure distances, spot hidden geometry |
| **Measuring Chain** | Whip, grapple, pull enemies in | Grapple across gaps, pull down obstacles |
| **The Map** | Draw cover, platforms and walls in combat, briefly | **Redraw Faded paths** to make them real; erase obstacles |

**The Map is the core mechanic.** The player can open it at any time. In the Fade, where everything is half-drawn, Ilse can draw paths, bridges and doors onto her map, and they become solid. She has limited ink. It refills at the Rest.

**The Margin edits the same map.** The map is a shared battleground. The Margin erases things Ilse has drawn, adds false routes, writes notes in the margins. In combat, it can erase the ground under Ilse's feet. In exploration, it redraws a corridor into a loop.

### Borrowed abilities

Ilse doesn't take gods. She **borrows** them. Each resident comes along on expeditions as a companion, and their god lends Ilse an ability:

| Resident | God | Lent ability |
|---|---|---|
| **Kit** | The Wall | **Bulwark**: raise a wall that blocks attacks and enemies; shelter behind it |
| **Margo** | The Beetle | **Small**: shrink to beetle-size to slip through cracks and under doors; dodge anything |
| **Dov** | The Starlings | **Murmuration**: release a flock that paints colour onto the Fade, making faded enemies solid and vulnerable |
| **Agathe** | The Ledger | **Name**: call out a Remnant's original name, which stuns it and reveals its weakness |

**Abilities get stronger as relationships deepen.** Evenings at the Rest are spent in conversation, cooking, repairing things, sitting with people. These raise the strength of their gods' abilities. (A small, honest version of the Persona social system: no gifts, no flirting optimization, just time spent.)

**And relationships can strain.** If Ilse skips evenings to work on her maps, which she'll be tempted to do, because the Margin will push her toward it, abilities weaken. Kit's Wall goes up and doesn't come down for a couple of days. Margo apologizes for bothering her and stops asking.

### Enemies: Remnants

In the Fade, the Stilled gods leave something behind: **Remnants**, hollow grey echoes of the gods that used to live there, wandering the Faded towns, repeating what they used to do. A Faded mill town is haunted by the Remnant of a foreman's god, still counting quotas for workers who no longer exist.

Every Remnant is a small tragedy. **Name** (Agathe's ability) reveals who they were.

### Death and respawn

When Ilse dies in the Fade, she wakes up in the Rest, in her room, and someone has put a blanket on her. Which resident it is depends on who you're closest to. The Margin is always in the corner:

> **THE MARGIN:** They had to carry you back. Again. Noting it.

## Part IV: The Story

### PROLOGUE: THE NARROWS

#### Scene 1: Morning

*Open on a rented room in the capital. Grey morning. Neat, almost empty. A drawing desk with maps pinned to the wall. Ilse is in bed, on her back, eyes open. She's been awake for some time.*

*In the margin of a map on the wall, in tiny handwriting, words are writing themselves:*

> **THE MARGIN:** They won't need you today.

*Beat.*

> **THE MARGIN:** Pryce will have your report by noon whether you deliver it or someone else does. The line will be drawn. The survey will happen. Nobody will be inconvenienced in any measurable way if Ilse Harrow does not, today, get up.

*Beat.*

> **THE MARGIN:** Noting it. No conclusion drawn.

*Ilse gets up. It's quick, practised. She's very good at this; the game doesn't make it hard. That's the point. The opening of **Weather** is the opposite of the other stories': there's no struggle to get up, because Ilse's depression doesn't look like that. She's up, dressed and out the door in thirty seconds of gameplay, and the player should leave the room thinking: she's fine.*

*She isn't. The rest of the game is about noticing that.*

#### Scene 2: The Survey Office

*The Crown Survey Office. Rows of desks. Pryce calls Ilse in.*

> **PRYCE:** The Rest district. Hob's Rest. Two miles south of the Narrows. *(he slides her a map with a pencilled red line)* The Order wants it inside the Quiet Zone. Line's a little crooked there. Straighten it, and it's a tidy report.

> **ILSE:** I'll survey it.

> **PRYCE:** Survey it, by all means. *(beat)* Mind, the Chief Cartographer post is up at the new year. I've your name on the list. Very tidy report, this one. Very tidy.

#### Scene 3: The bridge

*The Narrows: a deep gorge on the edge of the Fade with an old stone bridge across it. On the far side, everything is pale and half-drawn. The river below is grey.*

*Ilse sets up her theodolite. Takes her readings. Notes them. Gameplay: a short survey tutorial, teaching the Map mechanic. The player draws in a missing section of fence on the map and watches it solidify in the world.*

*She starts across the bridge. Halfway over, she looks down at her map.*

*The bridge on her map is being rubbed out. Slowly, from the far end. As if someone's using a pencil eraser on it.*

*The bridge under her feet starts disappearing, from the far end, coming toward her.*

> **THE MARGIN:** *(the handwriting in her margin)* Better this way. Tidier.

*Gameplay: a chase back along the vanishing bridge. The player has to redraw the bridge on the map as fast as it's being erased, a frantic tug-of-war with the Margin over a single drawing. It's impossible. The Margin is faster.*

*The bridge goes. Ilse falls into the grey river.*

*Underwater. Everything is pale and slow and quiet. Ilse isn't fighting it. That's what should frighten the player: she isn't fighting it. She's drifting, eyes open.*

> **THE MARGIN:** No one will need to look for you. That's a kindness to them.

*A huge hand, stone-coloured, closes on her collar.*

#### Scene 4: The Rest

*Ilse wakes up in a bed that isn't hers. Warm light. A room painted, every wall of it, in murals: birds, lots of birds, in orange and red and gold. A small iridescent beetle is sitting on the blanket next to her hand.*

> **THE BEETLE:** Sorry. Sorry. Sorry, I'm sorry, are you awake? Sorry.

*A woman in a cardigan comes in with soup and stops in the doorway.*

> **MARGO:** Oh! Oh, I'm so sorry, I didn't mean to — he's not supposed to be on the bed, I'm *so* sorry. Are you all right? You don't have to be all right, I just — sorry. Here's soup. Sorry.

*Downstairs, Agathe is making a mess of dinner in a kitchen painted bright orange. Dov is up a ladder painting the ceiling with his flock of ink starlings. Kit is sitting at the table, silent, huge, with a stone wall the size of a garden fence half-built around his chair.*

> **AGATHE:** Kit pulled you out. He doesn't talk much. Don't take it personally.

> **ILSE:** Where am I?

> **AGATHE:** Hob's Rest. *(she clocks Ilse's survey satchel by the door, and the Crown insignia on it, and her face changes slightly)* Ah. You're the line-drawer.

> **ILSE:** I'm the surveyor.

> **AGATHE:** Same thing, love, from where we sit. *(she puts a bowl in front of her)* Eat. Then you can come and condemn us.

### ACT ONE: THE SURVEY

#### The plan

*Ilse's leg is badly hurt in the fall. She can't walk back to the capital for weeks. She'll stay at the Rest, survey the district from here, finish the report and leave.*

*The household are very aware of what her report means. Nobody says it. They're kind to her anyway.*

> **THE MARGIN:** They're being kind because Agathe told them to be. And because they're frightened of what you'll write. Noting it.

#### The expeditions

*Act 1 is three expeditions, each a survey job into the Fade, each with a resident tagging along "to help", which is to say, to keep an eye on the line-drawer.*

**Expedition 1: Sallow Mill, with Kit.**
*A mill town that took Stilling, every resident, twenty years ago, to stop the Fade. It didn't stop the Fade. The town is entirely Faded now: pencil-sketch buildings, pale ghost-machinery, and no one living there at all. The Stilled moved away years ago, peacefully, to the capital.*

*The Remnants here are the hollow echoes of the town's old gods, still doing their jobs.*

**Boss: The Foreman.** *The Remnant of a mill overseer's god. A huge grey figure of clocks and tally-marks, still counting quotas for workers who no longer exist. It counts everything Ilse does. It grows when she's efficient. It's a remnant of **productivity as worth**, and it's the first enemy that makes Ilse uncomfortable, because it sounds like her.*

> **THE FOREMAN:** *(in a dead, chanting voice)* Eleven. Twelve. Not enough. Thirteen. Not enough.

*Kit's Wall is learned here. During the fight, Kit puts it up around himself and won't come out. Ilse has to finish the fight alone. Afterward, she sits down outside the Wall. She doesn't say anything. After a long time, Kit lowers a corner of it.*

> **KIT:** *(the first thing he's said to her)* Thanks for not knocking.

**Expedition 2: Thornbury School, with Margo.**
*Margo's old school, deep in the Fade. She wanted to see it. She doesn't say why.*

**Boss: The Register.** *The Remnant of a headmistress's god: a towering, grey, open register book that calls the roll for children who are long gone, and marks them absent. The Register can't be damaged while it's calling names. Margo's Beetle (Small) lets Ilse slip under its pages to reach its spine.*

*After, Margo sits in her old classroom and cries, quietly, and apologizes for crying, and apologizes for apologizing. Ilse, very awkwardly, sits next to her.*

> **MARGO:** Sorry. I'm so sorry, this is silly.

> **ILSE:** *(after a long pause)* You don't have to be sorry. *(beat)* I'm not good at this. I don't know what you're supposed to say.

> **MARGO:** *(laughs, wet)* No. Me neither. *(beat)* Isn't that a relief?

**Expedition 3: Gullhaven Light, with Dov.**
*A lighthouse on the Faded coast. Dov wants to paint it. He's been awake for three days.*

**Boss: The Keeper.** *The Remnant of a lighthouse keeper's god. A grey beam that sweeps the coast, still looking for ships. Anything caught in the beam is Faded further. Dov's Starlings are needed to paint colour into the beam's path and make solid ground.*

*Midway through, Dov's mania tips. His Starlings stop helping and start painting everything, wildly, flooding the fight with colour and chaos. Ilse has to finish the fight with Dov's god out of control. After, Dov sits on the lighthouse steps, shaking, coming down.*

> **DOV:** I'm sorry. I didn't mean — I can't always —

> **ILSE:** I know.

> **DOV:** How do *you* know?

*Ilse doesn't answer. The Margin, from the corner of her map:*

> **THE MARGIN:** You know because you'd rather be anywhere but here, too. You've just learned to look tidier about it.

#### Evenings

*Between expeditions: the Rest. Dinner. Agathe's terrible cooking. Dov painting. Kit's Wall going up and down. Margo's Beetle apologizing to the kettle.*

*At night, the player can walk around the Rest. Agathe sits by the fire with the Ledger open on her lap, reading one name a night. Out loud.*

> **AGATHE:** *(reading)* Thomas Avery. Brightwater. Spring of '31. His god was a fox. *(beat)* Goodnight, Tom.

*She closes the book.*

#### Midpoint: The Line

*Ilse finishes her survey. She sits down at the kitchen table to draw the final map of the Rest district.*

*And she can't get the line to close.*

*Her readings are wrong. They have to be. She takes them again. They're not wrong.*

*The Fade around Hob's Rest is **receding**. There's a ring of colour two miles across, centred on the house, and it's growing. Slowly. A few feet a month. The only place on the entire continent where the Fade is going backward.*

*She goes back through every survey she's ever done. Every Faded town she ever mapped. Sallow Mill went Faded within five years of taking Stilling. So did Thornbury. So did Gullhaven. Every Quiet Zone she's ever drawn Faded faster after the Order moved in, not slower.*

*Stilling doesn't protect against the Fade. It **causes** it. And the Loud, with their unbearable, unstilled gods, are the only thing on the map drawing the world back in.*

*She has the evidence. It's on the table in front of her.*

*The Margin appears in the kitchen for the first time, fully visible, standing in the corner. It's tall and thin and covered in tiny handwriting, and it's calm.*

> **THE MARGIN:** File it the way Pryce wants.

> **ILSE:** It's wrong.

> **THE MARGIN:** It's tidy. *(beat)* Consider the facts. You file the line straight. The Rest goes into the Quiet Zone. They're Stilled. They sleep. Kit never goes behind his wall again. Margo stops apologizing. Dov stops shaking on the lighthouse steps. Agathe stops reading out names at night. *(beat)* And you get your desk. You never have to come back here. Nobody needs you to.

*Long silence.*

> **THE MARGIN:** You could be in the capital by the new year. Away from all this. Noting it.

*There's a choice on screen. It isn't a dialogue choice. It's a drawing. The player has the red line on the map. They can draw it **straight**, Pryce's line, or they can draw it **true**, a ring of colour around the house.*

*The game lets them draw it either way.*

*If they draw it straight: Ilse files the report. The next morning, the player sees Agathe at the kitchen table, reading it. She doesn't say anything. She puts it down and goes and makes breakfast. The Margin has nothing to say. It doesn't need to. Then **Ilse goes back that night, alone, and redraws it true.** She can't do it. The game doesn't let her finish the wrong thing. It costs the player one in-game day and one awful breakfast.*

*Either way, Ilse files the true line.*

### ACT TWO: OFF THE MAP

#### Dismissed

*Pryce sends a letter. Ilse's survey is "rejected as methodologically unsound". She's dismissed from Crown service. The Rest district is placed inside the Quiet Zone regardless. The Order will come at the turn of the year.*

*Ilse reads the letter at the kitchen table. Everybody's watching her read it.*

> **THE MARGIN:** There it is. You chose them, and now you have nothing, and they still have nothing, and now there are five people with nothing instead of four.

> **AGATHE:** *(across the table)* Well. *(beat)* You'd better stay for supper, then.

#### The Loud Map

*The household come up with a plan, a desperate, silly one, mostly Dov's idea. If the Crown won't publish the true map, they'll make one themselves. Find every pocket of colour left on the continent, every place where the Loud live and the Fade is going back, and map them all. Print it. Give it to everyone.*

*Act 2's expeditions go further out, finding **pockets**: a cluster of musicians' houses in the hills, a fishing village nobody's ever Stilled, an island monastery of loud, quarrelling monks. Every one is a small vivid circle in the Fade, and every one is receding the Fade around it.*

*This is the most hopeful stretch of the game. It's a map that's slowly filling with colour.*

#### The Margin escalates

*And the Margin starts erasing Ilse herself.*

*Not her body. Her **place on the map**. Ilse's name starts disappearing from documents. A letter she writes to an old colleague comes back: ADDRESSEE UNKNOWN AT THIS ADDRESS. Ilse's maps have her own name rubbed off them. In a pocket village, she meets someone she worked with for ten years, and he doesn't recognize her.*

*At the Rest, Margo calls up to her room and then stands, confused, at the bottom of the stairs.*

> **MARGO:** I was going to... who was I...? *(beat)* Sorry. I've forgotten what I came in for.

> **THE MARGIN:** This is the kindest thing I can do for them. It's tidier. When you're gone, no one will have to miss you. I'm just making that easy.

*This is the cruellest stretch of the game, and its horror is quiet: social invisibility. The terror of being forgotten made literal, and the guardian's insistence that this is courteous.*

*Gameplay: the Margin's interference reaches its peak. It erases routes back to the Rest. Ilse's companions forget why they came on an expedition and wander off mid-dungeon. Abilities fade unless Ilse has spent real evenings with the residents.*

*The residents who remember her are the ones she's spent the most time with. The game's relationship system is now load-bearing for the plot: **the people who know you are the people you've let in.***

#### Agathe's Ledger

*Night. Ilse can't sleep. Agathe is up, by the fire, reading the Ledger.*

> **AGATHE:** Mary Delling. Thornbury. Summer of '38. Her god was a heron. *(beat)* Goodnight, Mary.

> **ILSE:** Why do you do that?

> **AGATHE:** Because I'm the only one who remembers them. *(she turns a page)* Two hundred and twelve. I did every one of them myself, you know. Told every single one of them it was a mercy. Some of them thanked me. *(beat)* I believed it. I really did. And then one morning I didn't, and I couldn't stop not believing it. So I left.

> **ILSE:** And opened a house.

> **AGATHE:** And opened a house. Two hundred and twelve, and then five. *(she looks at Ilse)* Six.

*She closes the Ledger.*

> **AGATHE:** I know what yours is doing to you, love. I've seen it before. I *did* it before. Rubbed people off the map and told them it was kind. *(beat)* It's not kind. I promise you. Whatever it says.

### ACT THREE: WEATHER

#### The Order arrives

*Midwinter. Warden Callis arrives at the Rest with twenty Stillers in white. He's here to Still the house. All of them.*

*Callis was Agathe's student. He loved her. He's never understood.*

> **CALLIS:** You trained me. Everything I know, Agathe. *Everything*. And then you walked off and you left me there, with them, doing it, and I never —

> **AGATHE:** I know, Tomas.

> **CALLIS:** It's a Quiet Zone now. It's the law. Please don't make me do this the hard way.

*The household are behind Agathe on the doorstep. Kit's Wall is half up. Margo's Beetle is apologizing frantically to everyone. Dov's Starlings are swirling. Ilse's Margin is standing in the doorway behind them, tall, quiet, watching.*

*Agathe steps forward.*

> **AGATHE:** Take mine, Tomas.

*She holds out the Ledger.*

> **AGATHE:** Two hundred and twelve, I owe you. Two hundred and thirteen, then. Take me, and give them a year. One year. Let the girl publish her map. If the Fade's still coming in a year, come back and take the lot of us, and I won't stand on the doorstep.

> **CALLIS:** Agathe —

> **AGATHE:** You were always such a good student, Tomas. Do it gently.

*There's no fight. The game doesn't let the player stop it. Ilse can try to step forward, and Kit holds her back by the shoulder. Agathe looks back at them all once and nods.*

*Callis Stills Agathe on her own doorstep. He does it gently. His hands are shaking.*

*The Ledger closes. Grey.*

*Agathe looks around her, pleasantly, at her house, at the painted walls, at the snow. She smiles at Callis, kindly, without recognition.*

> **AGATHE:** Would you like to come in for tea?

*Callis can't speak. He gives the order. The Stillers withdraw. One year.*

#### The storm

*That night, the Margin's attack finally comes.*

*Ilse is in the kitchen. Agathe is upstairs asleep. Kit is behind his Wall. Margo is in the corner, crying and apologizing for it. Dov hasn't stopped painting since afternoon, every wall, the stairs, the floor.*

> **THE MARGIN:** *(quietly)* This is your fault.

> **THE MARGIN:** You came here. You drew the true line. They lost their home and Agathe lost herself and it's all because you were here. They'd all be safe if you'd stayed in the capital. They'd be Stilled and safe and asleep and Agathe would still be Agathe.

> **THE MARGIN:** I'm going to fix it. I'm going to make it so you never came.

*The Margin opens Ilse's map. Draws an X over the Rest.*

*The house begins to fade.*

**Final Boss: The Storm.**

*The Margin rises. Up through the ceiling, into the sky above the Rest, and becomes **weather**: a vast storm system of map notation. Isobars swirling in the clouds. Wind arrows hammering the roof. Rain made of crossed-out names. Lightning in the shape of the red line. It's enormous, and it's erasing the house room by room.*

**This boss cannot be damaged.**

*The fight is about **survival and preservation**:*

- *Rooms of the Rest are being erased from the map. The player has to **redraw them** before they're gone, one at a time, in a frantic rush around the house.*
- *The residents are fading with the rooms. The player has to keep them on the map. Their gods help: Kit's Wall shields rooms from the wind. Margo's Beetle gets into collapsing spaces. Dov's Starlings repaint faded walls. The stronger the relationships, the more help they give.*
- *If the player **attacks** the storm, using the staff, the chain, anything aggressive, the storm gets worse. Faster erasing. Harder wind. Every attack feeds it.*
- *The storm goes on. And on. There's no timer, no health bar. Just preserving the house, room by room, as long as it takes.*

*The Margin talks the whole time. It's not shouting. It's still calm and factual, reading the facts:*

> *"You never wanted to be here. You wanted a desk."*
> *"Margo forgot your name last week. Noting it."*
> *"They'll be better off. Tidier. Let me tidy it."*
> *"You can't hold it all together. Noting it."*

**The storm ends when the player stops trying to make it end.**

*At some point, players will realize attacking makes it worse. When they stop, entirely, when they just keep redrawing, keep holding the house, the storm gradually slows. When all the rooms are redrawn and the player is doing nothing but holding, a new prompt appears on the map, in the corner, in the legend box:*

**[ ANNOTATE ]**

*Ilse opens the map to the legend. And instead of erasing the Margin, she **draws it in**. Adds it to the key.*

*She draws a small, scribbled storm cloud in the legend box. Next to it, in her careful, neat surveyor's handwriting:*

> **WEATHER.** *Frequent. Severe at times. Passes.*

*The storm stops.*

*Not gone. It shrinks. The isobars ease. The wind drops. The rain of names slows to a drizzle. The Margin comes down out of the sky and becomes small. A small grey cloud, about the size of a hat. It drifts down into the kitchen and settles in the corner of the ceiling above the stove.*

*It's quiet. It's still there.*

> **THE MARGIN:** *(very quietly)* Noting it.

*Ilse sits down on the kitchen floor. Kit's Wall comes down. Margo's Beetle crawls onto Ilse's hand. Dov sits beside her, covered in paint.*

> **DOV:** *(after a while)* Is it gone?

> **ILSE:** No.

> **DOV:** Oh.

> **ILSE:** *(looking up at the little cloud in the corner)* Overcast.

### EPILOGUE: THE ORDINARY MORNING

#### The Loud Map

*Spring. The Loud Map is finished. Dov painted the plates. Margo wrote the text, with many apologies. Kit carried the printing press up from the village. Ilse drew the map: every pocket of colour on the continent, and the ring around the Rest, now three miles across.*

*They print three hundred copies and give them away.*

*The game shows the year passing in a slow sequence: copies of the map passing hand to hand. In a pocket village, someone pins one up on a tavern wall. In the capital, someone reads it on a bench. A young Stiller in white, alone in a room, reads it twice and folds it into her robe.*

*The ring of colour around the Rest keeps growing. Other rings start appearing on the map. Small ones. People are leaving the Quiet Zones and moving toward the colour. New houses. New Rests.*

*The Fade is still there. The Order is still there. Callis comes back at the end of the year, as he promised. He reads the map for a long time at the kitchen table, and the Rest is outside the Fade now, and he leaves again without saying anything. He doesn't come back.*

#### Agathe

*Agathe is Stilled. She stays Stilled. Nothing brings her back; this isn't that story.*

*She lives at the Rest. She's calm and kind and pleasant and remembers nobody. The household look after her. Every night, Margo sits by the fire and reads her a name from the grey, closed Ledger, which Margo has been copying out by hand, one name a night.*

> **MARGO:** Thomas Avery. Brightwater. Spring of '31. His god was a fox.

> **AGATHE:** *(pleasantly)* What a nice name.

> **MARGO:** *(beat)* Goodnight, Tom.

*Ilse sits with them some nights. Agathe never knows who she is. Ilse sits with her anyway.*

#### The last scene

*Morning.*

*Ilse's room at the Rest. The same shot as the game's opening: she's on her back in bed, eyes open. She's been awake for some time. But the room is different. It's painted, every wall, in birds. There's a map on the wall that has her name on it. The light is warmer. Her palette is warmer. She's warmer.*

*In the corner of the ceiling, a small grey cloud.*

> **THE MARGIN:** They won't need you today.

*Same line, word for word.*

*Ilse looks up at it.*

> **ILSE:** Probably not.

*She gets up anyway.*

*The [ GET UP ] prompt from the opening appears. It's no easier than it was. It's no harder. It's a morning.*

*Downstairs: the kitchen. Margo's Beetle is apologizing to the toast for burning it. Kit's Wall is half up, but he's on the right side of it, eating porridge. Dov hasn't slept; he's painted the pantry door orange overnight and is looking at it uncertainly. Agathe is at the window, smiling at the garden.*

*Ilse pours tea. Five cups. She knows how everyone takes it.*

*The little grey cloud has drifted down from her room and is settling in the corner over the stove.*

> **DOV:** *(glancing at it)* Bad today?

> **ILSE:** *(sitting down)* Overcast.

> **MARGO:** Oh, sorry, I'll — do you want the window open? Sorry. I can —

> **ILSE:** Leave it. *(beat)* It passes.

*Hold on the kitchen. The light. Five people at a table. The weather in the corner.*

*Cut to black.*

## Part V: Why This Ending Works

**Nothing is cured, and that's honest.** *The Margin is still there in the last scene, saying the exact same line. Ilse isn't better in any magical way. She just answers. The game shows the difference between a voice that controls you and a voice you live with, and it's the smallest possible difference: "Probably not. Getting up anyway."*

**The final boss is the thesis.** *A boss that can't be beaten by attacking, and gets worse when you try. It's only resolved by naming it accurately, and placing it in context: "Weather. Frequent. Severe at times. Passes." The resolution isn't defeat. It's description.*

**The cost is real.** *Agathe is gone. She stays gone. The household's victory is paid for by its founder, and the game doesn't pretend otherwise. This keeps the ending bittersweet instead of saccharine.*

**Connection is mechanical, not decorative.** *The relationship system powers the abilities, and in Act 2 it decides who remembers Ilse at all. The game's entire structure makes the argument that you can't hold this alone.*

**The opening reversal.** *Most depression stories open on struggle. **Weather** opens on Ilse getting up easily, efficiently, and the player assuming she's fine. Her depression is the high-functioning kind, and the game makes the player notice it slowly, the same way she does. That's a portrait many players will recognize and few games have attempted.*

## Part VI: The Margin's Voice

*Every line is factual, calm and bureaucratic. It never insults.*

**Act 1**
> *"They won't need you today."*
> *"Third evening in a row you've eaten standing at the window. Noting it. No conclusion drawn."*
> *"They're being kind because Agathe told them to be."*
> *"You haven't said a word to anyone outside work in four years. Noting it."*

**Midpoint**
> *"File it the way Pryce wants. It's tidy."*
> *"Nobody needs you to come back. You could be in the capital by the new year."*

**Act 2**
> *"This is the kindest thing I can do for them. When you're gone, no one will have to miss you."*
> *"Margo forgot your name last week. Noting it."*
> *"Addressee unknown. Tidier this way."*

**The storm**
> *"I'm going to make it so you never came."*
> *"You can't hold it all together. Noting it."*

**The ending**
> *"Noting it."*
> *"They won't need you today."*

## Part VII: Production Notes

- *A **hub** game is significantly cheaper than a linear one: one detailed location re-used for 16 hours, plus smaller expedition dungeons.*
- *The **Map** mechanic is the core system and needs to feel great. Drawing a path and watching it solidify must be satisfying from the first minute.*
- *The **relationship system** can be very simple: a counter per resident, raised by spending evenings with them, unlocking ability levels and dialogue.*
- *Remnants are re-skinned basic enemies with a grey shader and a name.*
- *The **storm** final boss is mostly a level-state system (rooms being erased and redrawn) plus a weather effect. No complex boss AI needed.*
- *This is the most **writing-heavy** of the four stories. Evenings at the Rest are where the game lives.*
