# CLEAN LINES

> *The Order's most gifted god-hunter discovers that the god she Stilled at fifteen never died. It has been living in her reflection, starving, and now it wants out. She has one season to finish the job before anyone finds out. She succeeds.*

**Ending:** Pyrrhic victory. Maren kills her guardian completely and is made a saint of a calm, white, empty world. A hidden alternate ending exists for players who refuse the final button press.
**Length target:** 8–12 hours
**What it argues:** Silencing the voice completely isn't recovery. It's a death that looks like success.

---

## Part I: The Premise in One Paragraph

This is the dark mirror of the core concept: a **fall story**, told from inside the institution. The player plays as the Order's finest Stiller, the person the other stories' protagonists would be running from. She's competent, admired, and certain. She's also hollow, and doesn't know it, because she did it to herself at fifteen and hasn't felt the difference since. Her god comes back. She kills it. She wins. The game treats that win with complete seriousness, as both a triumph and a tragedy, and lets the player sit in it.

Critics should come out of this one disturbed by **how good it felt to play.**

---

## Part II: The People

### Maren Sollis (29)

**Stiller-Captain of the Order of the Still Hand.** The youngest captain in the Order's history. She has Stilled more gods than any living Stiller, and she does it with a technique only she has ever mastered: **the Clean Line**, a single perfect cut that Stills a god without a struggle. Hosts she Stills don't thrash or scream. They just exhale.

She was born in Hesk, a schoolteacher's daughter. A prodigy. Her scores were chalked on the classroom board every week, at the top. Her god was small and fierce and spoke in her father's classroom voice, and what it said, mostly, was one word:

> **"Again."**

She was drawing from the age of three: birds, mostly, and her mother. In colored chalk on the slate in her father's schoolroom. Her god used to make her redo them until they were right.

At fifteen she placed first in the Order's novitiate examinations, the highest mark in eighty years. That night, alone in the dormitory, her god said:

> **"It's not enough. Again."**

And Maren, at fifteen, with no rite, no supervision and no training, Stilled her own god with her bare hands. She's the only person in the Order's history ever to do it. It's the founding legend of her career. It's why she's going to be a saint.

**On the surface:** calm, exact, courteous, unhurried. Never cruel. Gentle with hosts, especially children. She believes completely that she is helping people, and in the narrowest sense she is.

**Underneath:** nothing. Or so she thinks. She hasn't wanted anything in fourteen years except to do her work well. She hasn't drawn since fifteen. She doesn't miss it. She has covered every mirror in her quarters with cloth, and if you asked her why she would say, honestly, that she doesn't know.

**Want:** to finish the job. To be what everyone already believes she is.
**Need:** to feel anything at all.
**What happens:** she gets what she wants.

**Visual design:** Maren is drawn in **perfect, clean, closed ink, completely uncoloured**: black lines on white. She's the most beautifully drawn character in the game, like a figure from an engraving. The player won't immediately notice she has no colour, because the game opens deep inside the Fade where nothing does. They'll notice gradually, when every other character in a vivid town has colour except her.

### The Chalk Girl (Maren's god)

**Design:** Maren at fifteen, drawn in **white chalk on black**, like a figure drawn on a school slate. Smudged where it moves. Constantly half-erased and redrawn. It lives inside reflective surfaces: mirrors, still water, polished steel, window glass at night. When it emerges, it climbs out of the reflection like chalk dust lifting off a board.

As the story progresses, it gains **colour**: coloured chalk, faint at first, a blue line here, a yellow smudge there. By the final fight it's in full colour, the most vivid thing in the entire game. These are the colours Maren used to draw with.

**Voice:** Maren's voice at fifteen. Precise, clipped, a teacher's cadence. It corrects. It drills. It's never cruel in tone, only relentless. Its favourite word is still "Again."

**What it is:** The Chalk Girl isn't only Maren's critic. It's everything Maren Stilled at fifteen: the critic, yes, but also the part of her that loved drawing, the part that cried at her mother's birthday, the part that wanted things. She thought she was cutting out the voice. She cut out the whole person. For fourteen years it has been living in mirrors, starving, and now it's strong enough to come out.

**Why it's trying to kill her:** it's not trying to hurt her. It's trying to **wake** her, with the only method it knows, which is relentless, punishing correction. It's a drill sergeant trying to shock a body back to life. Its logic is the same logic that ruined her childhood: *if you won't do it right, do it again until you do.* It just doesn't care any more if "again" kills her.

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** You've been asleep for fourteen years. Wake up. Wake up. *Again.*

### Brother Ansel Dray (54)

Maren's partner and the only friend she has. A senior Stiller, warm and shambling, the opposite of her in every respect. He was her warden in the novitiate, the one who found her the night she Stilled herself, sitting on the dormitory floor with chalk dust on her hands. He has watched over her ever since.

Ansel is the Order's best-kept secret: **he never Stilled his god.** He tried in his first year and couldn't bring himself to finish it. Instead he keeps it hidden in a small brass lantern-jar on his belt. **The Moth**: a little dusty grey moth, soft and anxious, that he lets out at night to fly around his room.

He's spent thirty years Stilling other people's gods while secretly keeping his own. He's ashamed of it. He's ashamed of the shame. He loves the Moth.

He's the person who could save Maren. He tries.

### Mother Severine Voss (60s)

The same Stillkeeper as in the other stories, but older and closer to the end of her life. She has known for decades that **every Stilling feeds the Fade**, that a world without arguments has nothing drawing it. She decided long ago it was worth it.

She has chosen Maren as her successor. She needs Maren for one thing: the **Still Accord**, a single Stilling reaching every god at once, carried out by the only Stiller whose technique is clean enough to do it, using the Clean Line.

In this story she knows nothing about the Chalk Girl. She sincerely believes Maren is what the legend says she is.

## Part III: The Shape of the Game

### How it plays

This should play differently from the other treatments. It's **precise, rigid and disciplined**, closer to Sekiro than Devil May Cry. Maren's moveset is small, exact and deadly. Every action has a precise frame window. She doesn't steal abilities: she learns **the Order's Five Cuts**, a traditional technique tree, unlocked by rank.

| Cut | What it does |
|---|---|
| **The Severing** | Basic strike. Fast, exact, short range. |
| **The Binding** | A tether that pins a god in place. |
| **The Hush** | A stun field. Gods inside it slow and fall quiet. |
| **The Mirror Ward** | A perfect counter that turns an attack back. |
| **The Clean Line** | Maren's signature finisher. Requires a perfect parry. Stills a god instantly. |

### Composure

Maren doesn't heal by connection the way characters in other stories do. She heals through **Composure**, a meter she fills by meditation at Order shrines, and by **Stilling gods**.

This is the game's dark engine. **Stilling is rewarded.** Every god Maren Stills makes her steadier, calmer, more powerful. Mechanically, the game teaches the player that the Order is right. The player will get good at Stilling, and will like it.

### The Chalk Girl's interference

The Chalk Girl interferes through **chalk** and **reflections**:

- **Exploration:** it writes on the world. Chalk arrows on walls that point the wrong way. Chalk doorways drawn on solid stone that open onto other rooms. A chalk "X" on a floor, and the floor disappears.
- **Combat:** it draws **sketches of gods Maren has Stilled**: hosts' gods redrawn in chalk, fighting her again.
- **Correcting:** in boss fights, it **marks Maren's inputs wrong**. A chalk "X" scratches across the screen over Maren's sword mid-combo, and the attack fails. Her technique is being graded and failed, live.
- **Reflections:** anywhere reflective is dangerous. It can reach out of mirrors, puddles, polished armour. Players learn to cover mirrors, avoid water, fight with their backs to stone.

**Adaptation:** like the guardian in every version of this story, it learns your habits. Unlike the others, it doesn't taunt. It **grades** you:

> *"Your parries are early. Again."*
> *"You favour the Binding. You always have. It's a crutch. I'm taking it."*
> *"C minus. Again."*

## Part IV: The Story

### PROLOGUE: THE KITE

#### Scene 1: The harvest fair

*Open on a harvest fair in the village of Thistle Cross, deep in the Fade. The fair is barely drawn: tents in faint pencil, people in pale grey ink, everything light and thin and quiet. Even the music from the fiddler is quiet.*

*Then: a scream. Overhead, a sixteen-year-old girl, **Dacey Rook**, is being dragged into the sky by her god, **the Kite**, a huge paper kite with a girl's face, its string wrapped around Dacey's wrist. It's swooping, ecstatic and terrifying, lifting her higher, wanting to carry her away from everything.*

*Down below, Dacey's father is shouting. The crowd is frozen.*

*Maren walks into the fair. She's not running. She's never running.*

> **ANSEL:** *(behind her, out of breath)* Mania, by the look. The kite's been building a week, father says. She hasn't slept.

> **MAREN:** Clear the green, please.

*The tutorial fight: Maren against the Kite. The player learns the Severing, the Binding (to pull the Kite down from the sky) and the Hush. The Kite is joyful and dangerous, all swooping attacks and paper wind. Dacey, still tied to it, is screaming the whole time, but whether it's terror or exhilaration is never clear.*

*When the Kite is pinned, the player gets the Clean Line prompt:*

**[ PARRY ]**

*Perfect timing. Maren's sword moves once, a single beautiful stroke that leaves a line of white light in the air, and the Kite simply goes still. Its paper folds in on itself. It drifts to the ground and becomes a small, grey, folded paper kite the size of a hand. Dacey drops into her father's arms.*

*She's not screaming any more. She's blinking. She looks around at the fair.*

> **DACEY:** Oh. *(beat)* I think I'm tired.

*Her father is sobbing with relief. He grabs Maren's hand.*

> **FATHER:** Thank you. *Thank* you. She hasn't — for a *week* she's —

> **MAREN:** She'll sleep tonight. She'll sleep every night now.

*Dacey looks up at Maren. Polite, gentle, a little blank.*

> **DACEY:** Thank you, Stiller.

*Ansel watches this. The player may notice he doesn't look happy.*

#### Scene 2: The mirror

*Night. Maren's quarters at the Order's waystation, small and spotless. Every mirror is covered with white cloth. She goes through her routine: removes her sword, polishes it, cleans the blade with oil, sets it on its rack.*

*She goes to wash her face. The basin of water is perfectly still. She looks down into it.*

*Her reflection looks back up at her, one frame late.*

*The player might miss it. The game's stepped animation means everything already moves in held poses, but the reflection is one frame behind the rest. It's the slightest stutter.*

*Maren washes her face. She looks into the water again.*

*Her reflection doesn't move. It's looking at her.*

*It's fifteen years old, and made of chalk.*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** You've been asleep a long time.

*The water erupts. Chalk-dust, a cloud of it, white and furious, pours out of the basin and fills the room. Chalk lines draw across the walls, the floor, the ceiling, grid lines like a slate, and the room becomes a blackboard. The covered mirrors drop their cloths one by one, and in every one of them there's a fifteen-year-old girl, drawn in chalk, reaching out.*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** Again.

#### Scene 3: The first attempt

*A fight inside Maren's quarters. The Chalk Girl comes out of every reflective surface: the mirrors, the polished sword on the rack, the washbasin, the dark window-glass. It hits hard and fast and every attack is a correction:*

> *"Your stance is wrong."*
> *"Your grip is wrong."*
> *"You've been doing it wrong for fourteen years. Again."*

*The player learns the Mirror Ward here, against a god that attacks from mirrors. It's also the first time the game's chalk X appears over Maren's sword mid-swing, cancelling the attack. The player is being graded and failed.*

*Maren survives only by smashing the mirrors. Every one. The last one breaks and the Chalk Girl screams, chalk dust exploding into the room, and then it's gone.*

*Maren stands in the wreckage, breathing hard. The player hears her breath for the first time in the game; she's been completely silent until now.*

*There's a knock on the door. Ansel.*

> **ANSEL:** *(through the door)* Maren? I heard glass.

*She looks at the room. Chalk dust everywhere. Every mirror broken.*

> **MAREN:** I dropped the basin.

> **ANSEL:** *(long pause)* All right. *(beat)* Goodnight, then.

*She sits on the floor among the broken glass. There is chalk on her hands. She looks at it.*

*She knows what this means. A Stilled god doesn't come back. Unless it was never Stilled. Unless, at fifteen, with no training, alone in a dormitory, she didn't do the thing everyone believes she did. Her whole career is built on a lie she told herself.*

*If the Order finds out, she's finished.*

*She'll have to kill it herself. Quietly. Before anyone notices.*

*Title card, drawn in chalk on black, then wiped clean by a single stroke:*

**CLEAN LINES**

### ACT ONE: THE HUNT

*Maren keeps working. The Order has contracts: feral gods in the provinces, dangerous and in some cases lethal. Maren takes them all. She's hunting gods by day and being hunted by her own god at night.*

*The structure of each region is the same: travel to a vivid province, meet a host whose god has gone feral, fight it, Still it. The Chalk Girl harasses her through reflections throughout. Maren starts carrying a dark cloth to cover water and windows.*

*Because each province is still vivid, the player begins to notice something: **every town Maren visits has colour, and Maren doesn't.***

#### Region 1: Brenning and the Furnace

*A smith's town, all sparks and smoke. **Jory Calder**, a blacksmith, has a god called **the Furnace**: a huge figure made of red-hot iron and coal, always angry, always stoking. He was a soldier once. The rage has been worse since he came home.*

*Jory doesn't want to be Stilled. His wife does want him to be. His children are frightened of the Furnace. His god has never hurt anyone, but it's come close.*

**Boss: The Furnace.** *A rage god. It gets **stronger every time it's hit**: each strike stokes it, building heat until it explodes. The correct way to fight it is **to stop attacking** and let it burn itself out. Maren's toolkit is all attack. The fight is hard because it asks the player to do something Maren never does. Players can brute-force it with perfect Clean Line timing. Most will.*

*The Chalk Girl's comment, from a puddle of water on the forge floor:*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** He's hurting. You can see him hurting. You used to know what that looked like. Look at him. *Look.*

*After the Stilling, Jory sits at the forge. His children come and sit next to him. He's gentle with them. He's always been gentle with them. He's just not anything else now. His wife thanks Maren, crying, and Maren can't tell whether they're happy tears.*

#### Region 2: Corvay and the Choir

*A cathedral town. The choirmaster, **Benedikt Arne**, has a god that is **a choir**: a dozen small, singing faces drawn in gold leaf, which sing aloud the private thoughts of everyone in the room. It began when his son died and he started hearing what everyone really thought of him.*

**Boss: The Choir.** *A paranoia god. It sings everything: the room's thoughts, the player's position, and above all the player's **next move**. The boss reads Maren's inputs and sings them aloud a moment before she makes them, then counters. To beat it, the player has to break their own patterns and fight unpredictably. The more predictable Maren is, the stronger the Choir becomes.*

*Halfway through the fight, the Choir starts singing Maren's thoughts. They're almost empty. There's very little there. It sings small repeated phrases:*

> *"Finish it. Finish it. Clean. Finish it."*

*And then, faint, under the rest, in a child's voice:*

> *"I want to draw the birds."*

*Maren falters. The Choir sings the line again, louder. Maren Stills it fast and hard, not cleanly at all, the Clean Line wobbling for the first time in the game.*

#### Ansel notices

*Back at the waystation after Corvay, Ansel finds her covering the window with her cloak.*

> **ANSEL:** You've covered every mirror you've passed for a month. *(gently)* And the windows. And the water.

> **MAREN:** I don't like reflections.

> **ANSEL:** Since when?

> **MAREN:** Since always.

> **ANSEL:** You were fifteen when you told me that, too.

*He takes the brass lantern off his belt. He opens it. A small grey moth flutters out, circles the room once, and lands on the lamp.*

*Maren goes completely still.*

> **ANSEL:** I tried, my first year. Couldn't finish it. *(he watches the Moth)* It's not much. It worries about things. It flies at lights. It's been thirty years and I still don't know what it wants from the lights.

> **MAREN:** You're unstilled.

> **ANSEL:** I'm unstilled. *(beat)* And I think you are too.

*He's not threatening her. He's offering.*

> **ANSEL:** There's other ways, Maren. You don't have to do it like the Order. You don't have to do it like you did when you were fifteen. You can keep it. Live alongside it. It's not easy. It's never easy. But I'll be here. I've been here all along.

*She doesn't answer. Very slowly, she reaches out a hand toward the Moth. It flutters to her fingertip. It sits there, delicate, trembling, grey.*

*She looks at it for a long time.*

> **MAREN:** I'll think about it.

*She hands it back to him.*

---

### ACT TWO: THE ARCHIVE

#### The Stillhouse

*Maren is summoned to the Stillhouse. Severine wants to see her. The Stillhouse is the whitest place in the game: perfect linework, no colour at all. Here, for once, Maren matches the world around her. She fits.*

*Severine tells Maren she will be named successor at the spring rites. Before then, she wants Maren to see something.*

#### The Archive

*Deep beneath the Stillhouse is **the Archive**: endless shelves of small glass jars, millions of them, running down into the dark. In every jar: a small grey thing. A folded kite. A cooled coal. A tarnished choir-face. Every god the Order has ever Stilled.*

> **SEVERINE:** They're not destroyed. We put them away. *(she holds up a jar to the light)* We used to think it was respectful. Now we know it's necessary.

*She explains: the world is drawn by the arguments between people and their gods. Every god in this Archive is an argument that has stopped. Every jar is a little ink that's no longer being added to the world. **Stilling causes the Fade.** The Order has known for three generations.*

> **MAREN:** Then we're killing the world.

> **SEVERINE:** Slowly. And very gently. *(she puts the jar back)* Maren. I was twelve when my god told me I'd be better off gone. I believed it. Do you understand? I believed it with my whole heart. If someone had come and Stilled it then, I'd have spent fifty years in peace instead of the six I spent terrified before I found the Order.

*She gestures to the endless dark shelves.*

> **SEVERINE:** A world of colour, and everyone in pain. Or a world of quiet, and no one in pain. You've seen the Furnace. You've seen the Choir. You've seen the Kite dragging a child into the sky. *(beat)* Pick.

*There's a long silence. The game holds on Maren's face.*

> **MAREN:** Quiet.

*Severine smiles at her, and it's the warmest smile in the game.*

> **SEVERINE:** I knew it would be you.

*She explains the Still Accord. A single Stilling, every god at once. All suffering, ended in a breath. It needs the cleanest technique there has ever been. It needs the Clean Line.*

*In a polished jar on the shelf, faintly, the Chalk Girl is watching. For the first time she has a smudge of colour in her: a single line of blue.*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** *(barely audible)* You used to draw the sky that colour.

#### Midpoint: The Moth

*Maren returns from the Stillhouse. Ansel is waiting. He's been waiting a long time, and he can see in her face what she's chosen.*

> **ANSEL:** You told her yes.

> **MAREN:** I told her yes.

> **ANSEL:** *(quietly)* And me?

*It's the Order's law: an unstilled Stiller must be Stilled. If Severine finds out about Ansel, it'll be done publicly, and his thirty years of work will be called a fraud. If Maren does it, it'll be done quietly, gently. Cleanly. No one will ever need to know.*

*Ansel understands this. He doesn't run.*

> **ANSEL:** Maren, I'm asking you not to.

> **MAREN:** I know.

> **ANSEL:** I'm asking you to — Maren. Look at me. I *found* you. On that floor, with the chalk on your hands. You were fifteen. You were so *proud*, and you were crying, and you didn't even know you were crying. *(beat)* I should have stopped you then.

> **MAREN:** You couldn't have.

> **ANSEL:** I could have tried.

*He opens the lantern. The Moth flies out. It circles the room. It flies toward Maren's lamp.*

**Boss: The Moth.**

*This is the gentlest boss in the game. **The Moth does not fight back.** It flutters. It circles. It flies toward the lamp, toward Maren, toward light. It has no attacks. It has a health bar.*

*The player has to kill it. There's no way past this point except through it.*

*The game gives no help. No music. No guidance. Just the Moth circling, the lamp, Ansel standing silently against the wall, and Maren's sword.*

*Players will hesitate. Many will put the controller down. The game waits. It will wait forever.*

*When the player finally does it, it takes a single Clean Line. Perfect. Effortless. The Moth folds into a small grey thing the size of a thumbnail. It drops onto the floor. Maren picks it up and puts it in a jar.*

*Ansel sits down. Slowly. His face smooths.*

> **ANSEL:** *(after a while)* Oh. *(beat)* That's much quieter.

*He looks at Maren. Pleasant. Kind. Gone.*

> **ANSEL:** I think I'll sleep tonight.

*Maren's Composure meter is full. It glows. It's the most powerful she's been in the game. A UI chime plays: a gentle, pleasant ding.*

*In the dark window, the Chalk Girl has turned away. She can't watch.*

### ACT THREE: AGAIN

*Maren is unstoppable now.*

*The final act is a **power fantasy played completely straight**. Maren is the most powerful character the player has ever controlled. Her Composure is endless. Her Clean Line is perfect. Every remaining feral god in the provinces falls in a single stroke. The game gives the player spectacular, stylish, effortless combat. Fights that took ten minutes in Act 1 take ten seconds. The Order's music swells.*

*And everywhere Maren goes, the world fades a little more behind her.*

*The Chalk Girl is everywhere now: every reflection, every puddle, every window. But she's not attacking any more. She's **pleading**. And with each region, she's more colourful: blue, yellow, then red, then green. She's drawing, on every reflective surface. Birds. Over and over again. Childish chalk birds, in colour. And every single one of them gets corrected. Smudged out. Redrawn. She can't stop it.*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** You used to love this. You'd stay up all night in Papa's classroom drawing birds. You'd draw them until your hands were blue. *Remember?*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** I'm sorry I made you do it again. I'm sorry I said it wasn't good enough. It was good enough. It was *good*. I just didn't know how to say anything else.

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** Please. Please. I'm all that's left of you.

*Maren doesn't answer. She covers every mirror, and she keeps walking.*

#### The final contract

*Spring. The rites. Severine is ready to perform the Accord with Maren as the nib. But before Maren can take the role of the Accord's instrument, the Order requires one thing: a demonstration. Maren is to perform the Clean Line on the most powerful god the Order has on record.*

*Severine doesn't know. But Maren does. The most powerful god on record is the one that's been living in her reflection for fourteen years.*

*Maren tells Severine the truth. Severine is silent for a while.*

> **SEVERINE:** Then finish it. *(gently)* And then come home.

#### The final boss: The Chalk Girl

*Maren goes back to Hesk, to her father's empty schoolroom. It's deep in the Fade, barely drawn: a few pale desks, a slate board on the wall. The slate is the only dark surface left in the village.*

*Maren stands in front of the board. Her reflection in the black slate, faintly, looks back at her.*

> **MAREN:** Come out.

*The Chalk Girl climbs out of the slate.*

*She's in full colour now, the only full-colour thing in the game for a long time, drawn in every colour of chalk Maren ever owned.*

**The fight has three phases.**

**Phase 1: The Critic.**
*The Chalk Girl fights the way she always has: correcting. The whole schoolroom becomes a giant slate. Chalk X's slash across Maren's moves. Phantom grades appear over the player's actions in chalk: "C". "D". "Again." Chalk sketches of every god Maren Stilled come out of the board and fight beside her. The Kite, the Furnace, the Choir. The Moth, small and grey, flutters at the edge of the arena, not fighting.*

> *"Again. Again. Your form's wrong. Again."*

**Phase 2: The Memory.**
*The schoolroom is redrawn as it was when Maren was six. In colour. Her childhood drawings are chalked all over the walls: birds, hundreds of birds, her mother's face, the sea, the sky in blue. The Chalk Girl isn't attacking any more. She's drawing. Every one of her attacks is a drawing: chalk birds that fly at Maren. They don't do damage, they just scatter into colour when they hit.*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** This is what you cut out. Not me. *This.* You didn't kill the voice that said "again." You killed the girl who drew.

*The Chalk Girl's health bar does go down, but slowly. Maren is cutting through her childhood drawings to reach her. Each drawing she cuts goes grey.*

**Phase 3: The Reach.**

*The schoolroom goes quiet. All the colour is back in the Chalk Girl now. Every grey drawing on the walls, every grey bird, every scrap of colour Maren cut through has gone back into her. She's so bright it's hard to look at.*

*She stops fighting.*

*She walks toward Maren, slowly. Arms out.*

*She doesn't attack. She's reaching for her.*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** Again?

*It's the same word, the word that ruined Maren's childhood, but it's not a command any more. It's a question. A plea. **Let's try again.***

*The prompt appears. The same one the player has seen in every single fight. The prompt they've hit a hundred times. The one they're very, very good at.*

**[ PARRY ]**

*The player is good at this. The game has trained them for this exact moment for ten hours. Every system in the game has rewarded this button.*

*Most players will press it.*

#### The Clean Line (canonical ending)

*Maren's sword moves once.*

*One perfect line, white light hanging in the air.*

*The Chalk Girl stops. She looks down at the line through her middle. She looks up at Maren.*

> **MAREN:** No.

*The Chalk Girl dissolves. Not violently. The colour drains out of her first, down from her head, until she's white chalk again, then grey, then pale grey, then nothing. Dust drifts down to the schoolroom floor.*

*Every colour in the room goes with her. Every drawing on the walls. The whole village beyond the window. The last colour in the game is gone.*

*Maren stands alone in a clean, white schoolroom.*

*She picks up a piece of chalk from the floor, the last one. She holds it up and looks at it, as if trying to remember what it's for.*

*She puts it down.*

#### Epilogue: Saint Maren

*The rites. The Stillhouse, perfectly white. Maren in white, Severine at her side, the Order assembled.*

*The Still Accord is performed. The game shows it in a single wide shot: Maren raises her sword, draws one line, and the light from it spreads across the continent. Every god, everywhere, goes quiet at once.*

*A city of a hundred thousand people sighs at the same moment. Everything is calm.*

*The epilogue is **playable**. The player can walk Maren anywhere in the capital. It's beautiful in a terrible way: pristine white streets, perfect linework, no colour anywhere. Everyone is pleasant. Everyone says hello. Every god walks beside its host, small and grey and silent. There are no enemies. There's nothing to fight. The music is soft, pleasant and has no melody.*

*There are things to find if the player looks:*

- ***Dacey Rook**, the girl with the Kite, is grown up, a clerk at a records office. She's kind. She says, "You helped me once, I think." She doesn't remember how.*
- ***Ansel** sits on a bench in the Stillhouse garden. He sleeps through every night. He says, "I'm very well, thank you, Maren." He has a brass lantern on his belt, and it's empty. He doesn't know why he carries it.*
- *A **child** in the street has a newborn god, tiny and grey, already Stilled at birth by the Accord. The mother says, "She's never cried. Not once. Isn't that lucky?"*
- *A **statue** of Maren in the Stillhouse square, inscribed THE CLEAN LINE. It's perfectly drawn and looks nothing like her.*

*Finally, if the player goes there, the last scene: Maren's childhood room in Hesk. Her old sketchbook is on the shelf. Every page is blank. The drawings have faded with everything else.*

*She turns the pages. Blank. Blank. Blank.*

*Maren looks at it for a long time. There's no prompt.*

#### The hidden ending: Chalk

*At the final prompt, if the player **does not press parry**, the game waits.*

*The Chalk Girl walks toward Maren very, very slowly. It takes three real minutes to reach her. The prompt stays on screen the whole time. The game doesn't help or hint. Every system the player has learned says press it.*

*If the player waits:*

*The Chalk Girl's hand touches Maren's face. It leaves a smear of blue chalk on her cheek.*

*And Maren's face gets colour. Just a little. Just where the chalk touched.*

*Maren drops her sword. She makes a sound she hasn't made in fourteen years. She's crying. She doesn't know how to do it; it comes out terrible and ugly and huge.*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** There you are.

*She puts her arms around Maren, and Maren lets her.*

*The hidden epilogue is short and quiet. It's ten minutes long, but it's the most hopeful ten minutes in the game.*

- *Maren breaks into the Archive. She finds one jar. A small grey moth. She takes it to Ansel and opens it in front of him. The Moth flutters out, confused, dusty, and lands on his lamp. Ansel's face changes. He starts crying. He starts laughing.*
- *They leave the Order together, at night. Maren in a plain coat, her face streaked with a little colour.*
- *Last shot: a vivid seaside town, somewhere at the edge of the map. Maren on the harbour wall, drawing birds in chalk on the stones. They're not very good. She's out of practice. The Chalk Girl stands behind her, looking over her shoulder.*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** *(critical, from habit)* The wing's wrong.

> **MAREN:** I know.

*She keeps drawing anyway. She doesn't erase it.*

> **THE CHALK GIRL:** *(after a while, grudging)* It's not bad.

*Cut to black.*

## Part V: Why This Ending Works

**It plays the power fantasy completely straight.** *The game never winks. Stilling is mechanically rewarding all the way through. The Composure system rewards every Stilling. Act 3's power fantasy is spectacular. The player should feel the pull of the Order's argument, because the argument is coherent and the game is fun. And then the game shows them, in its epilogue, exactly what they bought.*

**The voice is silenced, and that is the tragedy.** *This story doesn't argue that the critic is right. It argues that **destroying the critic outright destroys everything else with it**. Maren didn't just cut out "Again." She cut out the birds. The world's colour and the voice's cruelty turn out to be made of the same material.*

**The final button press is the thesis.** *A game that teaches the player that parry is the correct response to everything, then asks them to choose whether to parry a girl reaching for a hug. Most players will press it. The game doesn't punish them for it. It shows them what that choice cost, then shows a few of them, the ones who waited, what the alternative was. Critics will discuss the three-minute wait.*

**The Moth is the darkest moment, and it has to be.** *The player is made to kill something harmless, gently, by the person who loves them most. It's the game's clearest statement that the Order's mercy is a kind of violence.*

**No one is a villain.** *Severine's argument is sincere. Maren's choice is understandable. Ansel's cowardice is human. The Stilled are not monsters. The world is just quieter and worse.*

### Safety notes specific to this story

- *Severine's backstory ("it told me I'd be better off gone") is the most explicit reference to suicidal ideation in any of these stories. It is mentioned once, without detail, and is presented as the root of her tragedy, not as a justification the game endorses.*
- *Because the canonical ending is bleak, the hidden ending matters. It shows that the damage is reversible, even late. Consider surfacing its existence after the credits in a subtle way ("There is another way") so players who need hope can find it.*

- *The Archive is one modular shelf, instanced millions of times.*
- *The Chalk Girl's "correction X" in combat is a UI overlay plus an input cancel.*
