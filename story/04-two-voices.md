# TWO VOICES

> *A pianist who hasn't played in front of anyone since he was eight is offered a place in the most prestigious recital in the world. His god will do anything to stop him. You play both of them.*

**Endings:** Four, decided by how both halves of the player behave: **Encore** (coexistence), **Silence** (control), **Curtain** (tragedy) and **Duet** (secret).
**Length target:** 10–14 hours
**What it argues:** The player is the critic too.

---

## Part I: The Premise in One Paragraph

Every other treatment asks the player to fight the inner critic. This one asks them to **be** it. The game alternates between chapters as **Sef**, a shy pianist, and chapters as **the Prompter**, his god, who has spent eighteen years making sure no one ever sees him fail again, and who has concluded that the only certain way is to make sure no one ever sees him at all. As the Prompter, the player builds the traps, routes and ambushes that they then have to survive as Sef. They design their own suffering, and they learn why it felt necessary. How cruel the player is as the Prompter, and how violent they are as Sef, decides which ending they get.

Critics should come out of this one talking about the moment they realized they'd been the voice.

---

## Part II: The People

### Sef Adair (26)

**Pit pianist** at the Lantern, a shadow-play theatre in **Velle**, the city of theatres. He plays in the dark, under the stage, for audiences who never see him. He likes it that way. He tells everyone he likes it that way.

He has perfect pitch, and a strange gift because of it: **he hears gods as music**. Every god in Velle has a tune to him. A baker's god is a jaunty little waltz. A grieving widow's is a cello holding one low note. His own god, he's never heard. Or rather, he's always heard it: a whisper, just under hearing, like a prompter in the wings.

He's sweet, funny, self-deprecating to a fault, constantly humming. He makes jokes about himself before anyone else can. He practises the piano five hours a night, alone, in the empty theatre after closing, and has done for eighteen years, and has never once played for anyone.

At eight, at his first recital at **Halloway Hall**, he froze at the piano in front of three hundred people. He sat there for what felt like hours and was probably a minute. Then he wet himself. Then someone in the audience laughed, and then a lot of people did.

**Want:** to play the Grand Recital. He wants it so badly he can't say it out loud.
**Need:** to be seen.
**Flaw:** he would rather never try than risk being seen failing.

### The Prompter (Sef's god)

**Design:** a hooded figure made of **black stage curtain and sheet music**, lit from below like a figure in a prompt box. It carries a huge bound script: Sef's life, annotated in red. It moves with the soft, silent glide of a stagehand in blacks. Its face is a lamp, the dim hooded lamp in a prompter's box, and when it speaks, the lamp brightens.

**Voice:** a stage whisper. Intimate. Fast. Practical. It speaks in theatre: "Places." "Hold." "Cut." "Not yet." It's never cruel for the sake of cruelty. It's a stage manager in a crisis that has lasted eighteen years.

**Origin:** before Halloway, Sef's god was tiny and cheerful: a little **metronome** that ticked happily along when he practised and made him laugh. The night of the recital, in the wings, while three hundred people laughed, the metronome stopped ticking. It changed. It pulled a curtain around itself. It came out as the Prompter.

Its first words, in the dark, in the wings at Halloway, to an eight-year-old sitting in his own mess:

> **THE PROMPTER:** Never again. Never again will they see you. I promise. I'll make sure of it.

**What it believes:** if no one ever sees Sef, no one can ever laugh at him. Every action it takes for eighteen years follows from that promise. And the logic, followed all the way down, ends somewhere terrible. The Prompter has never let itself see that.

### Lio Marchetti (26)

Busker. Plays the trumpet outside the Lantern every evening, badly, with enormous joy. His god is **the Brass Monkey**, a chaotic little monkey made of tarnished brass that clashes cymbals at random, steals hats and scares horses. Lio is entirely unembarrassed by it. He's Sef's only friend. He's loud, warm, and forever trying to drag Sef out of the pit.

**His secret**, revealed late: Lio was at Halloway Hall that night. He was eight. He was due to play right after Sef, and he was terrified. When Sef froze, Lio laughed first. Out of nerves, out of relief that it wasn't him. He's known who Sef was since the day they met. He's been trying to make up for it for six years, and he's never told him.

### Hanne Ostrova (74)

Sef's piano teacher when he was a child. Once a great concert pianist. After a catastrophic performance thirty years ago, a memory lapse at the Grand Recital itself, she went to the Order and was Stilled. She still teaches: mechanically perfect lessons in a silent house. Her students learn every note exactly. None of them love it.

She's what the Silence ending looks like, living.

### Directress Odile Vance (50s)

Head of the **Velle Conservatory**, which runs the Grand Recital. The Conservatory works closely with the Order. Every performer in the Recital uses the Order's **Hush Fork**, a silver tuning fork that, struck, Stills a god for an hour. Long enough to perform. "Stage fright," says the Directress, "is a solved problem."

She heard Sef playing in the empty Lantern one night, from the street. She has offered him a place.

## Part III: The Shape of the Game

### Two players in one

The game alternates. Every area is played **twice**: first as **the Prompter**, then as **Sef**.

#### Prompter chapters: the stage

*The Prompter sees the world as a theatre. Its chapters are played from above, on a stage set. A city street becomes a painted flat on rails; a building is a set piece on a revolve. The player, as the Prompter, has a limited number of **Cues** to spend preparing the area Sef is about to walk through:*

| Cue | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Shift Scenery** | Move a wall, door, staircase or street. Reroute Sef. |
| **Place Understudy** | Set a copy of an enemy Sef has met somewhere along his route. |
| **Rewrite Line** | Change what an NPC will say to Sef. Change a sign. |
| **Drop** | Rig a set piece to fall. A sandbag, a lighting rig, a balcony. |
| **Steal a Line** | In a boss fight, give one of Sef's abilities to the enemy. |
| **Hold** | Do nothing. Leave it as it is. |

*The Prompter's objective for each chapter is written at the top of the screen, in red, as a stage direction:*

> *KEEP HIM FROM THE AUDITION.*
> *MAKE SURE NO ONE SEES HIM.*
> *DON'T LET HIM REACH THE HALL.*

#### Sef chapters: the street

*Then the player plays the same area as Sef, in third-person action, living with every choice they just made. Every wall they moved, every Understudy they placed, every falling set piece they rigged.*

**The player designs their own obstacles, then has to survive them.**

#### Why would anyone make it hard for themselves?

*Because the Prompter has **its own story**, and it's locked behind cruelty.*

*Between Prompter chapters, in the dark of the prompt box, the Prompter remembers. These memories are the game's backstory: Sef as a toddler with his little metronome god; his mother; Halloway Hall; the eighteen years since. They're beautiful, sad, and the only way to learn what really happened that night.*

*But **memories unlock with Grip**. Every Cue the player spends cruelly (every Drop, Understudy, Rewritten Line) raises the Prompter's Grip. High Grip unlocks memories. Holding back keeps Grip low and keeps the memories locked.*

*This is the game's central tension. **To understand your critic, you have to let it hurt you.** The player must decide how much.*

### Sef's tools

*Sef hears gods as music. His combat is **rhythmic**: attacks land harder on the beat of the music, which is the music of whichever god he's fighting. A rhythm-action hybrid, readable and stylish.*

**Resolving gods.** *When Sef defeats a feral god, he can **Resolve** it: play its own tune back to it, at the piano or by humming, until it calms. A short, hard rhythm sequence. The host keeps their god. Sef learns the god's **motif**: a new ability.*

**Hushing gods.** *Or he can use the Hush Fork the Directress gives him. One strike, instant, effortless. The god goes still for an hour. Sef also learns the motif. It's quicker and easier. There's no rhythm sequence. Most players will be tempted.*

*Every Hush adds to Sef's **Force**.*

| Motif | From | Combat | Exploration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cymbal** | Lio's Brass Monkey | Crash: stuns and staggers in a radius | Shatter brass and glass |
| **Pizzicato** | A dancer's god | Plucked, fast multi-hit | Wall-run on the beat |
| **Pedal** | A bell-ringer's god | Sustain: hold an attack's force and release it later | Ring bells to open gates |
| **Rest** | Hanne's Stilled god | A silence; everything stops for a beat (time-stop) | Pass through Prompter's scenery |

### The two hidden values

| Value | Raised by | Means |
|---|---|---|
| **Grip** | Cruel Cues as the Prompter | How much the critic controls Sef |
| **Force** | Hushing gods as Sef; Hushing himself before performances | How much Sef controls the critic |

*The game never shows these numbers. Players feel them. High Grip: the Prompter speaks more, Sef's chapters get darker, NPCs (rewritten by the Prompter) are crueller to him. High Force: Sef's chapters get quieter, the colour leaves, the music thins.*

## Part IV: The Story

### OVERTURE: HALLOWAY

#### Scene 1: The first cue (playable prologue, as the Prompter)

*The game opens not on Sef, but on **the Prompter's view**.*

*A dark stage, seen from above: Halloway Hall, eighteen years ago. A grand piano in a spotlight. Three hundred audience seats, rendered as rows of painted faces on flats. And a tiny figure at the piano, an eight-year-old boy in a little suit, completely still.*

*At the top of the screen, in red:*

> *HE'S FROZEN. EVERYONE IS LOOKING.*

*The boy isn't moving. In the audience, a sound starts: a single laugh, then more.*

> *MAKE THEM STOP LOOKING.*

*The player has one Cue: **Drop**. It's highlighted. The game's interface is pointing at the stage curtain.*

*The laughter grows. It's awful. The boy on the piano stool is crying.*

*The player drops the curtain.*

*It falls. Heavy, red, total. The laughter is cut off on the other side. Silence. Dark. Just the boy and the Prompter, behind the curtain, alone.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** *(to the boy, the first thing it ever says)* Never again. Never again will they see you. I promise. I'll make sure of it.

*It's the tutorial. It's also the moment the player becomes the voice. They did it to save a crying child. It was kind. It's the first Cue, and the player should feel completely justified.*

*Title card: two lines of text written in different hands, one neat, one hurried, both reading the same:*

**TWO VOICES**

#### Scene 2: Morning (as Sef)

*Eighteen years later. Sef wakes up in a small attic over the Lantern theatre. Sun through the window. He's humming before his eyes are open.*

*He goes downstairs into the empty theatre. The pit, the piano under the stage, the shadow-play screen above, the rows of empty seats. Gameplay: he practises. The player gets a short, simple rhythm sequence: a piece he's been working on for eighteen years. It's beautiful. He's very good. Nobody's listening.*

*From the street, the sound of Lio's terrible trumpet. Sef laughs.*

*Lio sticks his head in the stage door. The Brass Monkey is on his shoulder, stealing his hat.*

> **LIO:** You hear about the Grand Recital?

> **SEF:** Mm-hm.

> **LIO:** Auditions are on the twelfth.

> **SEF:** Good for them.

> **LIO:** Sef.

> **SEF:** I play for shadow puppets, Lio. I'm a shadow puppet's pianist. I've got a very niche set of skills.

*He says it lightly. Lio doesn't laugh.*

*At the theatre door, a letter has been pushed under. Gold-sealed. From the Velle Conservatory.*

> *Mr Adair. I heard you play from the street last Tuesday, after midnight. I would like to offer you an audition for the Grand Recital. — Directress O. Vance*

*Sef reads it four times.*

*In the wings, just at the edge of the stage, the lamp in a prompt box flickers on.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** *(whisper)* Places.

### ACT ONE: REHEARSAL

#### Chapter 1 (Prompter): "Keep him from the audition"

*The player is back above the city. Velle is a stage set now: painted flats, rails, a revolving stage under the market square. The route from the Lantern to the Conservatory is laid out below.*

> *KEEP HIM FROM THE AUDITION.*

*The player has eight Cues. They can reroute Sef's streets, place Understudies of street-thugs Sef has seen, rewrite what the market traders say to him, rig a lighting gantry over the Conservatory steps to fall.*

*The **first required Cue** is scripted: the game insists on one **Drop**, a sandbag over the stage door of the Lantern. The game won't let the player continue without placing it. The red direction:*

> *IF THEY CAN'T SEE HIM, THEY CAN'T LAUGH.*

*This is the assassination attempt. The player rigs it themselves.*

*Everything else in the chapter is optional. The player can Hold, leave it all alone, and the Prompter will say:*

> **THE PROMPTER:** Hold? *(beat)* You'd let him walk out there? To *them?*

#### Chapter 2 (Sef): The audition

*Sef leaves the Lantern for the audition. At the stage door, the sandbag the player rigged falls. Sef barely dodges. It hits the ground where he was standing and bursts.*

> **SEF:** *(staring up at the rigging)* ...That's not supposed to be there.

*From the prompt box at the side of the stage, a whisper, and for the first time Sef **hears** it clearly:*

> **THE PROMPTER:** Go back inside.

*The rest of the chapter is the route to the Conservatory, exactly as the player prepared it. Every scenery change. Every Understudy. Every rewritten line. If the player was cruel, the market traders say things like "Off to embarrass yourself?" If the player held back, the traders wish him luck.*

*At the Conservatory, Directress Vance meets him. Sef plays. Gameplay: an audition rhythm sequence. If the Prompter is at high Grip, the Prompter interferes during it: notes go missing from the sheet, keys rearrange.*

*Either way, Sef plays well enough. Vance offers him a place. She also offers him a small silver tuning fork, wrapped in velvet.*

> **VANCE:** Everybody uses one. Strike it before you go on, and your god sits quietly for an hour. *(she smiles)* Stage fright is a solved problem, Mr Adair.

*Sef holds the fork. He looks at it for a long time.*

#### Chapter 3 (Prompter): Memory

*If Grip is high enough after the first two chapters, the Prompter remembers.*

*A memory, played in the stage-set style: a tiny apartment, warm. Sef, three years old, at a toy piano. His god is a little brass metronome with a smiling face, ticking along. His mother, laughing, clapping in time.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** *(narrating, remembering)* I used to tick. That's all I did. He'd play, and I'd tick, and she'd clap, and he'd laugh so hard he'd fall off the stool. *(beat)* I was *good* at ticking.

#### Act 1 continues

*The act alternates through Velle's districts, with each area played first as the Prompter, then as Sef. Sef starts rehearsing at the Conservatory. Feral gods appear across the city, gods of other performers, cracking under the pressure of Recital season.*

**Boss: The Dancer's god.** *A Conservatory ballerina's god, **the Pointe**, a towering figure balancing on one impossible toe. Feral from starvation and perfectionism. It's all spins and sharp kicks. Its rhythm is a fast, frantic waltz.*

*Sef can Resolve it (a hard rhythm sequence, matching the waltz until the Pointe slows and comes down off its toe) or strike the Hush Fork (instant).*

*If Resolved, the ballerina sits on the floor and eats an orange for the first time in a week. If Hushed, she gets up, thanks Sef politely, and goes back to rehearsal.*

#### Hanne

*Sef visits his old teacher, Hanne Ostrova. Her house is completely silent: Stilled. She gives him a lesson, the way she used to. It's perfect, precise, correct and lifeless.*

> **HANNE:** Again, from bar sixteen. Your left hand is late.

> **SEF:** Do you still play? For yourself?

> **HANNE:** *(after a pause)* I don't see why I would.

*Hanne's god, a tall grey figure that was once, Sef remembers, a grand singing piano, stands in the corner, Stilled, silent. Sef can hear its music, very faintly. A single held note, very far away. Hanne can't.*

*Sef can learn the Rest motif from it, the only motif in the game that comes from a Stilled god. A silence. Time stops.*

### ACT TWO: HALLOWAY HALL (MIDPOINT)

*The Grand Recital's dress rehearsal is at Halloway Hall.*

*The same hall. Eighteen years later. Sef knows. The Prompter knows.*

#### Chapter (Prompter): "Drop the curtain"

*The player sees Halloway Hall from above as the Prompter. It's exactly the stage from the prologue: same piano, same seats, same red curtain.*

*And Sef is at the piano. For rehearsal. A handful of people in the seats.*

> *IT'S HAPPENING AGAIN.*

*The Prompter has Cues. All of them. Unlimited for the first time.*

*And the game reveals, in this chapter, the Prompter's final memory, available to every player regardless of Grip:*

#### The memory: what actually happened

*Halloway Hall. Eighteen years ago. Eight-year-old Sef at the piano, frozen. The laugh. The crowd laughing.*

*But this time the memory goes on past where the prologue cut off.*

*The curtain dropped. Behind it, the little boy crying. And on the other side of the curtain, the Prompter could hear the audience. They'd stopped laughing. A woman's voice: "Is he all right?" Another: "Poor thing." People were getting up. Sef's mother was running down the aisle. An usher was saying "Give him some air." Somebody was crying for him.*

*The audience had laughed for eleven seconds. The rest of the evening, they'd been worried about him.*

*The Prompter heard all of it.*

*It never told him.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** *(remembering)* They stopped. They stopped laughing. Eleven seconds. I counted. And then they — they *cared*, and I couldn't — if he knew they cared, he'd go back out there. And if he went back out there, it could happen again. *(beat)* So I didn't tell him. I kept the curtain down. Eighteen years. I kept the curtain down.

#### The midpoint choice (both halves)

*Now both halves of the player make a choice, one after the other.*

**As the Prompter** *(first)*: *the stage, Halloway, Sef at the piano for rehearsal. The red curtain. The player can:*

- ***Drop the curtain.*** *The Prompter's way. The hall goes dark. Sef will have to fight through Halloway as the Prompter attacks with everything.*
- ***Hold.*** *Don't drop it. Leave Sef in the light.*

**As Sef** *(second)*: *Sef at the piano at Halloway. The Prompter, curtain dropped or not, appears beside the piano for the first time in its full form: curtain, sheet music, lamp-face. It's here. It's real.*

*If the curtain dropped, it's attacking. If not, it's standing in the wings, shaking, waiting.*

*Sef can:*

- ***Fight it.*** *Boss fight: the Prompter. (The fight described below.)*
- ***Hush it.*** *Strike the fork. One hour of silence.*
- ***Play.*** *Ignore it entirely. Sit at the piano and play to the empty hall. (Only available if the player chose Hold as the Prompter.)*

#### Boss: The Prompter (if fought)

*The Prompter fights like the Prompter chapters play. It moves the scenery of Halloway Hall in real time. It drops set pieces. It sends out Understudies of every god Sef has met. It Steals Lines, taking Sef's motifs and turning them on him. It fights from the wings, never quite on stage. Its music is a whisper of the piece Sef couldn't play at eight.*

> *"Get off the stage!"*
> *"They're looking, they're looking at you, get OFF—"*
> *"I promised! I promised you, never again!"*

*If Sef wins, the Prompter retreats, wounded. It's still there. It's angrier. Grip rises.*

#### Lio

*After Halloway, whatever happened, Sef goes home. Lio is waiting at the stage door. He knows where Sef's been.*

*He tells him.*

> **LIO:** I was there. Halloway. I was eight. I was on after you. *(he can't look at Sef)* I'd been sick twice in the toilets. I was so scared. And you went out first and you just — sat there. And I was so relieved it wasn't me that I —

> **SEF:** You laughed.

> **LIO:** I laughed. I was *first*. *(beat)* I've known it was you since the day I met you. I've been — I don't know. Playing outside your theatre every night for six years, like an idiot. Like if I was loud enough, for long enough, it'd make up for it.

*Sef doesn't say anything for a long time.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** *(whispered, from the wings)* I knew. I always knew. That's why I tried to keep you away from him.

*Sef walks past Lio into the theatre. He doesn't say anything. The game cuts.*

*Next chapter, whatever the player chose, Lio is outside the Lantern playing his trumpet. Badly. With enormous joy. Sef comes out and sits on the step and listens, and doesn't say anything, and doesn't go back inside.*

---

### ACT THREE: PERFORMANCE

*The night of the Grand Recital.*

*The final act's chapters play through the hours leading up to it, alternating. The Prompter's Cues, the city, Sef's route to the Conservatory, the final performance. The ending depends on Grip and Force.*

#### The four endings

| | **Low Force** (Sef Resolved gods) | **High Force** (Sef Hushed gods / himself) |
|---|---|---|
| **Low Grip** (Prompter held back) | **ENCORE** *(or **DUET** if secret conditions met)* | **SILENCE** |
| **High Grip** (Prompter was cruel) | **CURTAIN** | **SILENCE** *(Force wins)* |

*Grip and Force are both visible in the game's feel, though never as numbers, and the game gives the player many chances to change course. No ending is a surprise to a player who's been paying attention.*

---

### ENDING: SILENCE

*(High Force: Sef chose control.)*

*Backstage at the Grand Recital. Sef in a borrowed tailcoat. The Prompter in the wings, shaking, pleading:*

> **THE PROMPTER:** Please don't go out there. Please. I'm begging you. They'll see. They'll —

*Sef takes out the Hush Fork. Strikes it.*

*A clean, high note. The Prompter goes quiet. Still. Grey.*

*And Sef keeps striking it. Again. Again. The note rings out, and it doesn't stop, and the Prompter shrinks with each strike. It isn't an hour of silence. It's permanent. The Directress, in the wings, sees him do it and nods approvingly.*

*Sef walks out on stage.*

*Gameplay: the Recital. A flawless rhythm sequence. Sef plays the piece he couldn't play at eight, perfectly, all the way through. No mistakes. No interference. Every note exactly where it should be.*

***The game's audio cuts out halfway through.*** *The music. Then the room tone. Then everything. The player is playing a rhythm sequence in total silence, hitting perfect notes they can't hear.*

*Sef finishes. He stands. He bows. The audience is on its feet. They're clapping. They're cheering. The player can see their hands moving, their mouths open.*

*There's no sound at all.*

*Sef looks out at the audience with a pleasant, polite expression.*

*Epilogue, short: Sef in Hanne's silent house, giving a lesson to a child. It's perfect and precise. The child is crying quietly. Sef doesn't notice.*

> **SEF:** Again. From bar sixteen.

*Cut to black. No music over the credits. Silence.*

---

### ENDING: CURTAIN

*(High Grip: the Prompter won.)*

*This is the darkest ending. It has to be handled with complete care. Sef's death is never shown directly, is never chosen by Sef, and is presented as pure loss. Nothing about it is peaceful.*

*The night of the Recital. Sef is backstage. The Prompter has never been stronger. It's everywhere: in the fly-loft, the rigging, the lighting gantries, the curtains. The player, in the final Prompter chapter, has prepared the Conservatory: every Drop, every Understudy, every wall moved.*

*The last red direction:*

> *THEY CANNOT SEE HIM IF HE ISN'T THERE.*

*The player has one final Cue left: a Drop. It's highlighted. It's over the stage. The Prompter's whole logic, followed all the way down, has arrived here.*

***And the game does not let the player press it.***

*The Prompter does it on its own. The player's cursor goes slack. The Prompter's hand reaches past it. The game makes the player watch the thing they built finish without them, and makes them understand the logic they fed is now running itself.*

*The fly-loft comes down. The stage goes dark.*

*The game does not show what happens to Sef. It cuts to a single image: the Prompter, standing alone on an empty stage, in the dark, under a single ghost-light, the bare bulb theatres leave burning overnight.*

*Final playable moment: as the Prompter. Alone. The player can move it around the empty stage. The Cues interface is open. There are no Cues left. There's no one to direct.*

*The Prompter goes to the prompt box. It opens its script, Sef's life. The pages are blank after tonight.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** Places.

*Nothing.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** Places. Places, please. *(beat)* Sef?

*It walks to the edge of the stage. Looks out at the empty hall.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** Nobody's looking. *(beat)* Nobody's looking at you now. I did it. I kept my promise.

*It sits down on the edge of the stage. Its lamp is dimming.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** I only wanted nobody to laugh. *(beat)* I didn't want nobody to *see.*

*The lamp goes out. The ghost-light stays on.*

*Credits over the ghost-light.*

***After the credits, the main menu has changed.*** *It's the empty stage, the ghost-light burning. The menu offers one new option:*

**[ RETURN TO HALLOWAY ]**

*It starts a new game from the Act 2 midpoint, with the memory of the eleven seconds already unlocked. The game is inviting the player to go back and try again, to keep the curtain up this time.*

*And under the ghost-light, the credits for every ending close with a line of text and a link to localized support resources:*

> *If you're hearing a voice like the Prompter's, you don't have to listen to it alone.*

---

### ENDING: ENCORE

*(Low Grip, low Force. The Prompter held back; Sef Resolved gods instead of Hushing them.)*

*Backstage at the Grand Recital. Sef in a borrowed tailcoat. The Prompter in the wings. It's shaking.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** Please don't go out there.

*Sef looks at the Hush Fork in his hand. The Directress is watching from the wings.*

*He gives it back to her.*

> **VANCE:** Mr Adair —

> **SEF:** I'd like to keep him.

*He walks over to the Prompter. It shrinks back.*

> **SEF:** They laughed for eleven seconds.

*The Prompter goes very still.*

> **SEF:** You knew. *(beat)* Lio told me he laughed. And I thought, how long did it go on? And I remembered. It wasn't long. And then there were people asking if I was all right. *(beat)* You never told me that.

> **THE PROMPTER:** If you knew, you'd go back out.

> **SEF:** Yeah.

> **THE PROMPTER:** And it might happen again.

> **SEF:** Yeah. *(beat)* Will you come with me?

> **THE PROMPTER:** *(after a long time)* I'll be in the wings. I'm always in the wings.

*Sef walks out on stage. The lights. Three thousand people.*

*Gameplay: the Recital rhythm sequence. It's hard. It's the hardest in the game. And the Prompter is still there. Every few bars, it whispers from the wings. Sometimes it's useful ("left hand, left hand"), sometimes it's frightened ("they're looking at you, they're all looking").*

*And **at one point in the sequence, the game forces a mistake**. A note drops. The player's input simply doesn't register, scripted, unavoidable. It's audible. A wrong note in a quiet passage. A cough in the audience.*

*The Prompter, in the wings, freezes.*

*The rhythm sequence keeps going. The player has to keep playing.*

*And the Prompter, from the wings, says:*

> **THE PROMPTER:** ...Keep going.

*Sef keeps going.*

*He finishes. It's not perfect. There was a wrong note. He stands. He bows.*

*The audience is clapping. The player can hear it. Lio is in the gallery, standing on his seat, hollering. Hanne Ostrova is in the third row. She's clapping politely, the way the Stilled do. But her foot, the game lets the player notice, was tapping in time.*

*Sef looks into the wings. The Prompter is in its prompt box. It's smaller than it was. Its lamp is bright.*

> **THE PROMPTER:** *(whisper)* Encore.

### ENDING: DUET (secret)

*(Low Grip, low Force, **and** at the midpoint the player chose Hold as the Prompter and Play as Sef: ignored the fight, sat at the Halloway piano and played to the empty hall.)*

*The night of the Grand Recital. Sef is backstage. The Prompter is in the wings.*

*Sef doesn't go on.*

*He gives the Directress back the fork. He apologizes to her. He means it. He walks out of the Conservatory with the Prompter, in his tailcoat, past the queue of three thousand people going in.*

*He walks across Velle to Halloway Hall.*

*Halloway is derelict now, a little hall the city's forgotten about. Dust on the seats. The old piano is still there under a sheet. Lio has the key, somehow. He's waiting on the steps. A few people are with him: Hanne, the ballerina from Act 1, a handful of the hosts whose gods Sef Resolved, a few of Lio's busker friends. Twenty people, maybe, in a hall built for three hundred.*

*Sef pulls the sheet off the piano. Sits.*

*The Prompter stands in the wings. Sef looks over at it.*

> **SEF:** Come on.

> **THE PROMPTER:** I don't —

> **SEF:** You used to tick. *(beat)* You were good at it.

*The Prompter walks out of the wings. On to the stage. Into the light. It's never been in the light before. Its curtain-body starts to fall away, and underneath it, small and brass and battered, there's a metronome.*

*It sits down at the piano next to Sef. On the bass end.*

*Gameplay: **a duet**. The player controls both hands: right stick and right buttons for Sef's melody, left stick and left buttons for the Prompter's bass. It's a rhythm sequence for two people played by one. It's not hard. It's the piece Sef couldn't play at eight, arranged for four hands.*

*The Prompter is clumsy at first. It hasn't played in eighteen years. It's never played. It ticks along, keeping time, and slowly it starts to play the left-hand part. It makes mistakes. Sef makes mistakes. The game lets them. They keep going.*

*They finish.*

*Twenty people in a dusty hall. Lio is crying. Lio is crying so much the Brass Monkey is patting his head. Hanne, Stilled, is sitting very still in the second row.*

> **HANNE:** *(pleasantly, as she always does)* That was very nice.

*But her god, the grey Stilled piano in the corner behind her, has a single note sounding. Very faint. Sef can hear it. The player can hear it. A held note, far away.*

*Last shot: the empty Halloway stage, after everyone's gone. The piano with the sheet off. On the piano lid: a small battered brass metronome, ticking.*

*Cut to black on the tick.*

## Part V: Why These Endings Work

**The player is complicit, and that is the point.** *Every cruelty Sef suffers in this game, the player chose. They rigged the sandbag. They rewrote the market traders' lines. They placed the Understudies. And they did it, first, to save a crying child. The game makes the player feel the critic's logic from inside: protective, justified, escalating, until it arrives somewhere unforgivable.*

**Grip makes understanding expensive.** *The player wants the Prompter's memories. To get them, they have to let the Prompter hurt Sef. It's the most honest model of the inner critic the game could build: you can understand it only by indulging it, and every time you indulge it, it gets stronger.*

**The eleven seconds.** *The central revelation is small and devastating. The audience laughed for eleven seconds and then cared about him for the rest of the night. The critic's whole eighteen-year case was built on hiding that. It's a true thing about how shame works: it remembers the laugh and forgets everything after.*

**No ending is "right," but they aren't equal.** *Silence is perfect and deaf. Curtain is horror. Encore is imperfect and alive. Duet is the hidden argument: that the critic, at its root, was once a metronome. That it can be brought into the light and taught to keep time.*

**Curtain is built to refuse its own logic.** *The game doesn't let the player press the last button. The Prompter does it on its own, which shows how the critic's logic, once fed, runs without you. Sef's death is never shown and never chosen by Sef. The final scene is the Prompter's horror, not peace. The Prompter's last line rejects everything it argued. Afterward, the game invites the player back to try again, and every ending's credits point to real support.*

### Safety notes specific to this story

- *The Curtain ending is the most dangerous content in all four treatments. If anything in it doesn't survive a lived-experience read, cut it. Encore and Duet carry the game without it.*
- *Consider making Curtain require **deliberate**, sustained high-Grip play, so no player reaches it by accident.*
- *The support line belongs in every ending's credits, not only Curtain's.*

## Part VII: Production Notes

- *This is effectively **two games**: a third-person rhythm-action game and a top-down level-editing strategy game. That's the scope problem. It's the most original concept of the four and the most expensive.*
- *The cheapest way to build the Prompter mode: it's a **level editor**. Every Cue is a pre-placed toggle at a pre-placed spot in each level. The player isn't free-building, just choosing which prepared traps to switch on. The Sef chapter then loads with those toggles set.*
- *The rhythm combat needs a solid music-sync system early. Every god needs a motif; every boss needs a track.*
- *The endings are mostly cutscenes plus one rhythm sequence each. The Duet's two-handed rhythm sequence needs the most bespoke work.*
- *If scope forces a cut: keep the prologue Cue and the midpoint as the only Prompter-playable chapters, and make the rest of the game Sef-only. The core idea (you are the critic) still lands with just those two.*
